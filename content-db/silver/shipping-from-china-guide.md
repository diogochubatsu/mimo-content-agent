## Executive Summary

It's survival.

This guide breaks down every major shipping method from China — air freight, sea freight, express courier, and rail — with real 2026 pricing, weight/volume calculation methods, customs clearance steps, and a case study using actual numbers. Whether you're shipping 50 phone cases or 50,000 LED strip lights, you'll find the data you need to make the right decision.

## Section 1: Shipping Methods Overview

### 1.1 The Four Main Methods

There are four primary ways to ship goods from China to global destinations in 2026:

| Method | Transit Time | Cost per kg | Best For |
|--------|-------------|-------------|----------|
| **Air Freight** | 5–10 days | $4–$10/kg | Medium-weight, time-sensitive |
| **Sea Freight (FCL)** | 25–40 days | $0.30–$2/kg | Large, heavy, non-urgent |
| **Sea Freight (LCL)** | 30–45 days | $1–$4/kg | Small-medium volumes |
| **Express Courier** | 3–7 days | $15–$25/kg | Samples, small parcels, urgent |
| **Rail (China–Europe)** | 15–22 days | $2–$6/kg | EU-bound, medium urgency |

### 1.2 How to Choose the Right Method

The decision matrix depends on three factors:

1. **Weight and volume** — Heavy, compact goods favor sea; light, bulky goods may need air or express
2. **Urgency** — Need it in 5 days? Express. In 5 weeks? Sea freight
3. **Budget** — The tighter the margin, the more likely sea freight wins

**Quick decision guide:**
- **Under 5 kg, urgent:** Express courier
- **5–50 kg, moderate urgency:** Air freight or express
- **50–500 kg, flexible timeline:** Air freight (consolidated) or LCL sea
- **500+ kg, no rush:** FCL sea freight
- **Shipping to EU, medium urgency:** China-Europe rail

### 1.3 Key Incoterms That Affect Your Shipping Cost

Incoterms define who pays for shipping, insurance, and customs. Understanding them is critical:

| Incoterm | Who Pays Shipping | Who Pays Customs | Risk Transfer |
|----------|-------------------|------------------|---------------|
| **EXW (Ex Works)** | Buyer | Buyer | At supplier's warehouse |
| **FOB (Free on Board)** | Buyer from port | Buyer | When loaded on vessel |
| **CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight)** | Seller to port | Buyer | When loaded on vessel |
| **DDP (Delivered Duty Paid)** | Seller | Seller | At buyer's door |

**Pro tip:** For beginners, FOB is the most common and safest choice. You control the shipping, and your supplier handles loading.

### 2.4 Volumetric Weight (DIM Weight) — The Hidden Cost

Air carriers charge by **volumetric weight** when your cargo is bulky but light. The formula:

```
Volumetric Weight = (L × W × H in cm) / 6000
```

**Example:**
- Actual weight: 50 kg
- Box dimensions: 120 cm × 80 cm × 80 cm
- Volumetric weight: (120 × 80 × 80) / 6000 = **128 kg**
- Carrier charges for: **128 kg** (not 50 kg)

At $6/kg, that's **$768** instead of $300 — a **156% increase**.

**How to avoid DIM weight surprises:**
1. Pack tightly — fill empty space
2. Use smaller boxes when possible
3. Get quotes using volumetric weight, not actual weight
4. Consider sea freight for large, lightweight items

## Section 3: Sea Freight — The Bulk Shipping Champion

### 3.1 When to Use Sea Freight

Sea freight is the workhorse of international trade. Over 90% of global cargo moves by sea. Use it when:
- You're shipping more than 100 kg
- Transit time of 3–6 weeks is acceptable
- You want the lowest per-unit shipping cost
- Your products are heavy or dense

## Step-by-Step Guide: How to Ship from China

### Step 1: Calculate Your Total Shipping Needs (Day 1)

Before requesting quotes, determine:

1. **Total weight** (kg) of your order
2. **Total volume** (CBM or dimensions in cm)
3. **Product value** (for insurance and customs)
4. **Destination address** (exact — rates vary by zip code)
5. **Timeline** — when do you need it?

**Calculation worksheet:**

```
Total Weight: _______ kg
Box Dimensions: _______ × _______ × _______ cm × _______ boxes
Total Volume: _______ CBM
Volumetric Weight (air): (L × W × H × boxes) / 6000 = _______ kg
Declared Value: $_______
```

### Step 2: Get 3+ Quotes (Days 1–3)

Contact at least three freight forwarders for comparison:

**Recommended platforms for quotes:**
1. **Freightos** — Instant online quotes, multiple carriers
2. **Flexport** — Full-service, tech-forward
3. **ShipBob** — Good for e-commerce
4. **Local freight forwarders** — Often cheapest for specific routes

**What to include in your quote request:**
- Product description and HS code
- Exact weight and dimensions
- Pickup address (supplier warehouse)
- Delivery address
- Preferred shipping method
- Any special requirements (temperature control, hazmat, etc.)

### Step 3: Choose Your Shipping Method (Day 3)

Based on quotes received, evaluate:

| Decision Factor | Weight |
|-----------------|--------|
| Total cost (not just shipping) | 40% |
| Transit time | 30% |
| Reliability/track record | 15% |
| Customs support | 10% |
| Insurance options | 5% |

**Selection criteria:**
- If total landed cost difference is under 10%: choose the faster method
- If timeline is critical: express or air, regardless of cost
- If you have 4+ weeks: sea freight almost always wins

### Step 4: Prepare Documentation (Days 3–5)

Essential shipping documents:

1. **Commercial Invoice** — Accurate product descriptions, values, quantities
2. **Packing List** — Itemized contents, weights, dimensions per box
3. **Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air)** — Contract of carriage
4. **Certificate of Origin** — Proves goods are made in China
5. **HS Code Classification** — Determines tariff rates
6. **Insurance Certificate** — Proof of coverage (if purchased separately)

**Common documentation mistakes:**
- Inaccurate declared values (leads to customs holds)
- Missing HS codes (causes delays and penalties)
- Inconsistent weights between documents (triggers inspection)

### Step 5: Arrange Customs Clearance (Days 5–7)

Work with a customs broker to handle clearance:

**Customs clearance process:**
1. Submit all required documents to broker
2. Broker files entry with customs authority
3. Customs reviews and may inspect shipment
4. Duties and taxes assessed and paid
5. Shipment released for delivery

**Customs broker fees (2026):**
- US: $150–$300 per shipment
- EU: €100–€250 per shipment
- UK: £80–£200 per shipment

**Tip:** Many freight forwarders include customs brokerage in their service. Always confirm what's included in your quote.

### Step 6: Track and Receive (Ongoing)

1. **Track your shipment** using the tracking number provided
2. **Monitor customs status** — delays are common, especially during peak season
3. **Arrange last-mile delivery** if not included in your shipping service
4. **Inspect goods immediately** upon arrival — document any damage within 48 hours
5. **File claims** for damaged or lost goods promptly

## Case Study: Real Numbers, Real Results

### Background

Sofia runs a growing Amazon FBA business selling LED strip lights and phone cases. She sources from Shenzhen and ships to the US (Los Angeles). In early 2026, she was shipping everything by express courier and losing margin on every order.

### Challenge

| Product | Monthly Units | Unit Cost | Selling Price | Express Shipping/unit |
|---------|---------------|-----------|---------------|----------------------|
| LED strips | 500 | $5.00 | $18.99 | $8.50 |
| Phone cases | 300 | $2.50 | $12.99 | $6.20 |

**Monthly shipping costs with express:** (500 × $8.50) + (300 × $6.20) = $4,250 + $1,860 = **$6,110**

After Amazon fees ($4/unit average), her margin was barely positive:
- LED strips: $18.99 − $5.00 − $8.50 − $4.00 = **$1.49 profit** (7.8% margin)
- Phone cases: $12.99 − $2.50 − $6.20 − $4.00 = **$0.29 profit** (2.2% margin)

### Solution

**Phase 1: Ship consolidation (Month 1)**
- Combined LED strips and phone cases into single air freight shipment
- Total weight: 350 kg (LEDs 200 kg + cases 150 kg)
- Switched from express to consolidated air freight

**Phase 2: Sea freight trial (Month 2)**
- Placed larger orders (2 months of inventory) and shipped by sea
- FCL not needed — used LCL (1.8 CBM)
- Increased inventory buffer from 2 weeks to 8 weeks

**Phase 3: Hybrid model (Month 3)**
- 80% of volume by sea freight (restocking every 6 weeks)
- 20% by air freight (fast-moving SKUs and new product launches)
- Kept express only for samples and emergency orders

### Results

| Metric | Before (Express) | After (Hybrid) | Savings |
|--------|-------------------|----------------|---------|
| **Monthly shipping cost** | $6,110 | $1,450 | **$4,660 (76%)** |
| **LED strip shipping/unit** | $8.50 | $1.20 | $7.30 |

### Key Takeaways

1. **Shipping method choice directly impacts profit margin** — Sofia's margin jumped from 5.5% to 35% by optimizing shipping alone
2. **Sea freight + air freight hybrid** is the optimal model for most e-commerce sellers
3. **Larger orders less frequently** dramatically reduce per-unit shipping costs
4. **Express courier is only for emergencies** — never as the default shipping method
5. **Inventory planning is a shipping strategy** — ordering 8 weeks of inventory instead of 2 enabled sea freight

## FAQ

**Q: What is the cheapest way to ship from China in 2026?**
A: Sea freight (FCL) is the cheapest per-kg option at $0.30–$2/kg, but requires a full container (approximately 20,000 kg). For smaller orders, LCL sea freight ($1–$4/CBM) or consolidated air freight ($4–$8/kg) offer the best value. Express courier ($15–$25/kg) is the most expensive but includes door-to-door delivery and customs brokerage.

**Q: How do I calculate volumetric weight for air freight?**
A: Use the formula: Volumetric Weight = (Length × Width × Height in cm) / 6000. Carriers charge for whichever is greater: actual weight or volumetric weight. For example, a box measuring 100×60×60 cm has a volumetric weight of 60 kg. If your actual weight is only 30 kg, you'll be charged for 60 kg.

**Q: How long does sea freight from China take?**
A: Transit time depends on the destination. From Shanghai: 14–18 days to US West Coast, 28–35 days to US East Coast, 28–35 days to EU ports, and 35–45 days to Brazil. Add 3–5 days for customs clearance and 2–5 days for inland delivery.

**Q: Do I need a customs broker to import from China?**
A: For commercial shipments over $2,500, yes — a customs broker is strongly recommended. They cost $150–$300 per shipment but prevent costly delays, misclassification penalties, and compliance issues. For small personal imports under $800 (US de minimis), no broker is required.

**Q: What is the difference between FCL and LCL?**
A: FCL (Full Container Load) means you rent an entire container (20ft or 40ft). It's cheaper per kg for large shipments (1,000+ kg). LCL (Less than Container Load) means your cargo shares a container with other shipments. It's more expensive per kg but only requires a minimum of 1 CBM or 100 kg.

**Q: Can I track my shipment from China?**
A: Yes, all major shipping methods offer tracking. Express courier (DHL, FedEx, UPS) provides real-time door-to-door tracking. Air freight offers airway bill tracking. Sea freight provides Bill of Lading tracking with port-by-port updates. Rail freight offers tracking through the rail operator's system.

**Q: What happens if my goods are damaged during shipping?**
A: First, document the damage with photos immediately upon delivery. Then file a claim with your insurance provider within 48 hours. If you purchased cargo insurance (0.3–0.8% of value), you'll be reimbursed for the damaged goods. Without insurance, you may have limited recourse depending on the carrier's liability terms.

**Q: How do I reduce shipping costs from China?**
A: Key strategies: 1) Consolidate orders (ship more, less frequently), 2) Optimize packaging to reduce DIM weight, 3) Negotiate volume discounts with freight forwarders, 4) Use sea freight instead of air for non-urgent orders, 5) Avoid peak season (Oct–Jan) surcharges, 6) Consider China-Europe rail for EU destinations.

**Q: What are peak season surcharges?**
A: From October to January (Golden Week through Christmas), shipping rates increase 20–40% due to high demand. Air freight is most affected. To avoid surcharges: order by August for holiday inventory, or accept higher costs and plan for them in your pricing.

**Q: Is shipping insurance worth the cost?**
A: Absolutely. Cargo insurance typically costs 0.3–0.8% of declared value — for a $10,000 shipment, that's $30–$80. A single lost or damaged container can cost thousands. Most experienced importers consider insurance non-negotiable, especially for sea freight where damage risk is higher.
