### Supplier Evaluation Checklist

1. **Rating:** 95%+ positive reviews required
2. **Order volume:** 100+ completed orders (validates reliability)
3. **Response time:** Under 12 hours
4. **Shipping:** Clear delivery timeframes with tracking
5. **Returns:** Defined return/refund policy
6. **Payment protection:** Trade Assurance or escrow available
7. **Communication:** Responsive in English (or your language)

## Supplier Red Flags to Avoid

| Red Flag | What It Means | Risk Level |
|----------|--------------|------------|
| Subscription fees beyond platform norm | Possible scam or overcharging | High |
| "Wholesale prices" offered to consumers | Not a real B2B supplier | High |
| Minimum order sizes conflicting with dropshipping | Not a dropship-friendly supplier | Medium |
| No Trade Assurance or buyer protection | No recourse if things go wrong | High |
| Response time over 24 hours | Poor communication, potential issues | Medium |
| Stock photos only (no real product images) | May not actually have the product | High |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. What is the best dropshipping supplier for beginners?
Shopify Collective (free with Shopify), DropCommerce (US/Canadian suppliers, free plan), and AI Dropship (free plan with 35 products) are the most beginner-friendly. They have simple interfaces, free plans, and verified suppliers.

### 2. Can I use multiple suppliers for one store?
Yes, and it's recommended. Use US suppliers for bestsellers (fast shipping) and China suppliers for long-tail products (lower cost). Apps like DSers and AutoDS support multi-supplier setups.

### 3. How do I handle orders from multiple suppliers?
Use automation tools like DSers, AutoDS, or CJDropshipping that integrate with Shopify. They automatically route orders to the correct supplier and provide unified tracking.

### 4. Are AliExpress suppliers reliable?
Many are, but quality varies. Always check: 95%+ rating, 100+ orders, 4.5+ stars, and order samples before listing products. Use DSers for automated order processing.

### 5. What is the difference between a supplier and a manufacturer?
A manufacturer creates the product. A supplier sources or resells products. Most dropshippers work with suppliers (who buy from manufacturers). Some suppliers, like Alibaba manufacturers, sell directly.

### 6. How much do dropshipping suppliers charge?
Most supplier apps are free or have free plans. You pay per order: product cost + shipping. Typical per-order costs range from $5–$25 depending on product and supplier location.

### 7. Should I use US or China suppliers?
Use US/EU suppliers for bestsellers (3–5 day shipping) and China suppliers for niche products (lower cost, 7–20 day shipping). A hybrid approach balances speed and margins.

### 8. How do I negotiate better pricing with suppliers?
Build volume first (50+ orders/month), then negotiate. Request volume discounts, loyalty pricing, or exclusive arrangements. Long-term partnerships get 10–20% better rates.

### 9. What happens if a supplier ships a defective product?
Contact the supplier immediately with photos. Most reliable suppliers offer replacements or refunds. Your store's return policy should cover this scenario. Budget 3–5% of revenue for defective product costs.

### 10. Can I switch suppliers after listing products?
Yes, but it takes effort. Update product listings, test the new supplier with samples, and gradually transition. Don't switch all products at once — test with low-volume items first.

### 11. Do suppliers handle customs and import duties?
International shipments may incur customs duties. Suppliers typically ship DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) for some countries. Clarify this before listing products for international customers.

### 12. How do I verify a supplier is legitimate?
Check: platform verification badges, years on platform, transaction history, business license, customer reviews, and request a live video audit. Order samples to verify quality firsthand.
