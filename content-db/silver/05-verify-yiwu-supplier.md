## How to Verify Yiwu Supplier Step by Step

### Step 1: Check the Chinese Business License

A legitimate supplier should provide Chinese business license copy, company registration number, legal representative name, business address, and business scope.

#### What to Look for on a Business License

| Field | What to Check | Red Flag |
|-------|--------------|----------|
| Company name (公司名称) | Matches what supplier claims | Name mismatch or abbreviation |
| Unified Social Credit Code (统一社会信用代码) | 18-digit number, verifiable online | Invalid or missing code |
| Legal representative (法定代表人) | Should be a real person | Fake or generic name |
| Registered address (住所) | Should match factory/office address | Address in residential building |
| Business scope (经营范围) | Should include manufacturing/trading | Scope doesn't match products |
| Registration date (成立日期) | Company should be established 2+ years | Very new company |

#### How to Verify a Chinese Business License Online

1. Visit the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (www.gsxt.gov.cn)
2. Enter the company name or unified social credit code
3. Verify the registration details match what the supplier provided
4. Check for any administrative penalties or abnormal operations
5. Confirm the company status is "Active" (存续) or "Normal" (在营)

### Step 2: Verify Company Name and Bank Account

Before sending payment, always confirm the bank account matches the registered company name. This is one of the most critical verification steps.

#### Bank Account Verification Process

1. **Request bank details**: Ask for bank name, account number, account holder name, and bank branch
2. **Match company name**: The account holder name must exactly match the registered company name
3. **Verify bank branch**: The bank branch should be in the same city as the registered address
4. **Check account type**: Corporate accounts (对公账户) are more reliable than personal accounts
5. **Confirm SWIFT code**: For international transfers, verify the correct SWIFT code

**Critical Warning**: Never send payment to a personal bank account unless you have a verified, long-standing relationship with the supplier. Personal account payments are the most common method used in supplier scams.

### Step 3: Request Factory Photos and Videos

Ask suppliers to provide production line videos, warehouse photos, staff working videos, machine photos, and packaging process videos.

#### What to Request and Why

| Request | What It Proves |
|---------|---------------|
| Production line video (unedited) | Actual manufacturing capability |
| Warehouse photos with inventory | Real stock availability |
| Staff working photos | Actual employees and operations |
| Machine photos with serial numbers | Equipment ownership verification |
| Packaging process video | Current order handling capability |
| Factory exterior with signboard | Physical location verification |
| Business license displayed in office | Legitimate business operation |

### Step 4: Conduct a Video Call Inspection

During the video call, ask them to walk through the office, request warehouse demonstrations, check production areas, and confirm sample products.

#### Video Call Inspection Checklist

- ☐ Walk through the office (continuous, no cuts)
- ☐ Show business license on the wall
- ☐ Visit the production floor (show active production)
- ☐ Display warehouse inventory
- ☐ Show the products you ordered (confirm they exist)
- ☐ Introduce key team members
- ☐ Show packaging area
- ☐ Confirm factory exterior and surroundings

#### Red Flags During Video Call

- Supplier refuses or constantly delays video calls
- Video call shows only one room or limited areas
- Background looks like a showroom, not a factory
- No active production visible during the call
- Supplier cannot show the specific products you ordered
- Video quality is suspiciously high (may be pre-recorded)
- Sound doesn't match the environment (studio audio in factory setting)

### Step 5: Order Samples Before Mass Production

Never place large orders without testing samples first. Sample ordering is your most important risk reduction tool.

#### Sample Order Best Practices

1. **Order from multiple suppliers**: Compare 2–3 suppliers for the same product
2. **Pay for samples**: Free samples may not represent actual product quality
3. **Request production samples**: Ask for samples from actual production, not showroom samples
4. **Test thoroughly**: Check dimensions, materials, functionality, packaging, and durability
5. **Document everything**: Take photos, measurements, and notes for comparison
6. **Keep samples**: Store approved samples for future reference and quality comparison

## Warning Signs You Should Never Ignore

### Prices Too Cheap
If one supplier offers prices far below market level, there is usually a reason. Extremely low prices may indicate:
- Counterfeit or substandard materials
- Missing safety components
- Reduced product specifications
- Bait-and-switch pricing (low price to attract, higher price for actual order)

### Refusing Video Calls
If a supplier constantly refuses live video calls, you should investigate further. Legitimate suppliers welcome video calls as proof of their operations. Refusal may indicate:
- The supplier is not a real factory
- The "supplier" is actually a middleman pretending to be a factory
- The operation is too small or unprofessional to show

### Pressure for Immediate Payment
Scammers often push buyers to transfer money urgently. They use tactics like:
- "This price is only available today"
- "Other buyers are interested in the same products"
- "We need the deposit immediately to start production"
- "Our bank account will change tomorrow"

**Rule**: Never rush a payment decision. A legitimate supplier will give you time to verify and prepare.

### Inconsistent Information
Watch for discrepancies between:
- Company name on business license vs. what they told you
- Factory address vs. registered address
- Products claimed vs. products shown in video
- Pricing vs. market average
- Communication style vs. claimed company size

### Other Red Flags

| Red Flag | What It May Indicate |
|----------|---------------------|
| No physical address provided | Fake or virtual office |
| Only personal bank accounts available | Not a legitimate business |
| Refuses to provide business license | Illegal or unregistered operation |
| Prices change frequently | Unstable or opportunistic supplier |
| No export experience | Cannot handle international orders |
| Poor English communication | May use translators to hide issues |
| No quality certifications | Products may not meet standards |

## FAQ

### How long does supplier verification take?
Basic verification (business license check, video call, sample order) takes 1–2 weeks. Comprehensive verification including factory audit takes 2–4 weeks. The time investment is minimal compared to the risk of losing thousands of dollars.

### Can I verify a supplier without visiting China?
Yes, you can verify most aspects remotely through business license checks, video calls, sample orders, and third-party inspection services. However, visiting China provides the highest level of verification and is recommended for large orders.

### What if the supplier's business license is expired?
An expired business license is a serious red flag. It may indicate the company has been shut down, is not maintaining proper registrations, or is operating illegally. Do not proceed with an unlicensed supplier.

### How do I check if a Chinese business license is real?
Use the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (www.gsxt.gov.cn) to verify the license. Enter the company name or unified social credit code to check registration status, legal representative, and any penalties.

### Should I trust suppliers with Alibaba Gold Supplier status?
Gold Supplier status indicates the supplier has paid for a premium membership and passed basic verification. It is a positive signal but not sufficient alone. Always perform additional verification steps.

### What payment methods are safest for first-time orders?
For first-time orders, use Alibaba Trade Assurance, Letter of L/C, or escrow services. Avoid direct bank transfers to unverified suppliers. After establishing a track record, you can transition to T/T payments.

### How many suppliers should I verify before choosing one?
Verify at least 2–3 suppliers for the same product. This gives you comparison points and reduces the risk of choosing the only option available. Compare pricing, quality, communication, and verification results.

### What is the difference between a factory and a trading company?
A factory manufactures products directly. A trading company buys from factories and resells. Factories typically offer lower prices and more control, but trading companies may offer better service and flexibility. Always verify which type you are dealing with.

### Can I trust suppliers who offer free samples?
Free samples may not represent actual production quality. Suppliers sometimes send their best samples rather than production samples. Always request production samples and be willing to pay for them to ensure you receive representative quality.

### What should I do if I suspect a supplier is fake?
Stop all communication and payment immediately. Document everything you have (messages, photos, payment records). Report the supplier to the platform (Alibaba, 1688) and consider filing a report with local authorities. If you used a sourcing agent, ask them to investigate.

### How often should I re-verify existing suppliers?
Re-verify suppliers annually or whenever you notice changes in quality, communication, or pricing. Business conditions change, and a previously reliable supplier may become unreliable over time.
