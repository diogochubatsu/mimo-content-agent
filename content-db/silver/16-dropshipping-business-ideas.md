## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1. What are the best dropshipping products to sell in 2026?
Phone accessories (90%+ margins), pet products (emotional buyers), beauty products (repeat purchases), and home organization (growing demand) are the most profitable categories.

### 2. How do I choose from 100+ ideas?
Use the P.I.C.K. framework: Profit (30%+ margin), Interest (genuine passion), Competition (differentiation possible), Knowledge (understand the market). Score each idea 1–5 on these criteria.

### 3. Can I sell in multiple categories?
Start with one category to build expertise. Add complementary categories once you've established your first niche (e.g., phone accessories → electronics → home gadgets).

### 4. What products have the highest margins?
Webcam covers (93%), hair accessories (94%), stickers (96%), phone cases (90%), and pet toys (87%) have the highest margins. Focus on lightweight, low-cost items.

### 5. How many products should I start with?
10–20 products in your chosen category. This allows focused marketing without overwhelming operations. Scale to 50–100 products once you identify winners.

### 6. Should I sell trending or evergreen products?
Both. Evergreen products (60–70% of inventory) provide stable revenue. Trending products (20–30%) provide revenue spikes. Seasonal products (10–20%) capitalize on holidays.

### 7. What is the easiest product category to start with?
Phone accessories, hair accessories, and pet toys are the easiest — lightweight, high margins, low competition, and simple to source from AliExpress.

### 8. How do I validate a product idea?
Order samples, run a $50–$100 Facebook ad test, check Google Trends for demand, and research competitor pricing. If click-through rate >1% and conversion >1.5%, the idea has potential.

### 9. Can I start with no money?
You can start with $0 during Shopify's free trial and use free supplier apps (DSers, AI Dropship). However, you'll need $50–$200 for marketing to drive traffic.

### 10. What products should I avoid?
Watches (extremely saturated), generic clothing (fast fashion dominance), health products without certifications, and products with high return rates (sizing-dependent items).
