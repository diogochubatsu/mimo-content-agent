## Why Use a Yiwu Sourcing Service?

A professional Yiwu sourcing service provides:
- **Local expertise**: Knowledge of Yiwu Market's 75,000+ shops and supplier landscape
- **Language bridge**: Chinese-English communication for effective supplier negotiations
- **Quality assurance**: Professional inspection and verification services
- **Logistics management**: Consolidation, packing, and international shipping
- **Risk reduction**: Supplier verification, payment protection, and dispute resolution
- **Time savings**: Eliminate the need for personal presence in China

## Step 1: Product Brief and Inquiry

The first step in any Yiwu sourcing project is the product brief. A strong brief should include product photos, dimensions, materials, packaging requirements, MOQ targets, destination country, and any certification requirements.

### What to Include in Your Product Brief

| Element | Why It Matters | Example |
|---------|---------------|---------|
| Product photos | Visual reference for suppliers | Clear images from multiple angles |
| Dimensions | Size specifications | 30cm × 20cm × 10cm |
| Materials | Material requirements | Food-grade silicone, BPA-free |
| Packaging | Packaging specifications | Retail box with barcode |
| MOQ target | Order quantity expectations | 500–1,000 units |
| Destination | Shipping and compliance needs | USA, needs FCC certification |
| Certification | Legal requirements | CE, FCC, UL as applicable |
| Budget | Price expectations | Target $2–3 per unit |

### Tips for Writing a Strong Brief

1. **Be specific**: Vague briefs lead to inaccurate quotations
2. **Include photos**: Visual references are more effective than text descriptions
3. **Specify quantities**: Help suppliers provide accurate pricing
4. **Mention compliance**: Identify certification requirements early
5. **Set timeline expectations**: Communicate your delivery deadline

## Step 2: Booth Shortlist and Supplier Vetting

Supplier verification is one of the most important parts of Yiwu sourcing. Verification should include:
- Business license verification
- Factory or showroom address confirmation
- Bank account verification
- MOQ capability confirmation
- Packaging and compliance review

### Supplier Vetting Checklist

- ☐ Business license received and verified online
- ☐ Company name matches all documents
- ☐ Bank account is corporate and matches company name
- ☐ Factory or showroom address confirmed
- ☐ Product samples available
- ☐ MOQ confirmed for your requirements
- ☐ Export experience verified
- ☐ Communication is professional and responsive
- ☐ Pricing is within market range
- ☐ No pressure tactics used

## Step 3: Quote Collection and Comparison

A structured Yiwu sourcing workflow normally collects multiple supplier quotations for each SKU. We recommend comparing at least three quotations whenever possible.

### Quote Comparison Template

| Comparison Item | Supplier A | Supplier B | Supplier C |
|----------------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Unit price | $ | $ | $ |
| MOQ | | | |
| Sample available | Y/N | Y/N | Y/N |
| Lead time | days | days | days |
| Payment terms | | | |
| Packaging included | Y/N | Y/N | Y/N |
| Export experience | Y/N | Y/N | Y/N |
| Business license verified | Y/N | Y/N | Y/N |

### How to Evaluate Quotations

1. **Compare like-for-like**: Ensure all quotes include the same specifications
2. **Check what's included**: Packaging, testing, and shipping may or may not be included
3. **Consider total cost**: Factor in agent fees, shipping, and duties
4. **Evaluate supplier reliability**: Price is not the only factor
5. **Ask for clarification**: Get written confirmation of all terms

## Step 4: Sample Approval

Once quotations are shortlisted, the buyer usually approves supplier samples before production begins.

### Sample Approval Process

1. **Order samples from top 2–3 suppliers**: Compare quality objectively
2. **Pay for samples**: Free samples may not represent actual quality
3. **Document specifications**: Record measurements, materials, and quality
4. **Test thoroughly**: Check functionality, durability, and appearance
5. **Approve in writing**: Get written confirmation that samples are approved
6. **Keep approved samples**: Store for future quality comparison

### Sample Cost Expectations

| Product Type | Typical Sample Cost | Production Time |

| Small accessories | $5–$20 | 3–5 days |
| Phone cases | $10–$30 | 3–7 days |
| LED products | $20–$50 | 5–10 days |
| Kitchen utensils | $15–$40 | 5–10 days |
| Bags/luggage | $30–$80 | 7–14 days |
| Electronics | $50–$200 | 7–21 days |

## Step 5: Order Placement and Deposit

Standard practice usually includes a deposit before production and balance payment before shipment.

### Payment Structure

| Payment Stage | Typical % | When Paid | Purpose |
|--------------|----------|-----------|---------|
| Sample payment | 100% | Before samples | Verify quality |
| Production deposit | 30% | Before production | Start manufacturing |
| Balance payment | 70% | Before shipping | Complete order |

### Payment Safety Tips

- Never pay 100% upfront for production orders
- Use corporate bank accounts, not personal accounts
- Consider Trade Assurance or escrow for first-time orders
- Keep all payment receipts and documentation
- Verify bank account details before every transfer

## Step 6: Production Monitoring

During production, the sourcing partner monitors manufacturing progress and identifies delays.

### What to Monitor

- Production timeline adherence
- Material sourcing and quality
- Manufacturing progress at key milestones
- Quality control during production
- Packaging preparation
- Ready-for-shipment confirmation

### Monitoring Milestones

| Milestone | Expected Timeline | Action if Delayed |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Material sourcing | 3–5 days | Request update and new timeline |
| Production start | Day 5–7 | Confirm production has begun |
| Mid-production | Day 10–15 | Check progress and quality |
| Production complete | Day 15–30 | Verify quantity and quality |
| Packaging complete | Day 20–35 | Confirm packaging matches specs |
| Ready for inspection | Day 25–40 | Schedule pre-shipment inspection |

## Step 7: Pre-Shipment Inspection

Pre-shipment inspection is one of the highest-value protection steps in any Yiwu sourcing workflow.

### Inspection Types

| Inspection Type | When | Cost | Best For |
|----------------|------|------|----------|
| During production | Mid-production | $150–250 | Large orders, new suppliers |
| Pre-shipment | Before shipping | $100–200 | Most orders |
| Container loading | During loading | $100–200 | Full container orders |

### What Inspection Covers

- Product quality and specifications
- Quantity verification
- Packaging compliance
- Labeling and marking
- Functionality testing
- Safety compliance
- Defect classification

## Step 8: Consolidation, Shipping, and Export Documents

One major advantage of Yiwu sourcing is the ability to combine multiple suppliers into one shipment.

### Consolidation Process

1. **Receive goods**: All supplier orders arrive at consolidation warehouse
2. **Quality check**: Verify each shipment against order specifications
3. **Repack if needed**: Consolidate into export-ready packaging
4. **Loading**: Optimize container space for cost efficiency
5. **Documentation**: Prepare export customs documents
6. **Shipping**: Arrange international freight

### Export Documents Required

| Document | Purpose | Required For |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| Commercial invoice | Customs declaration | All shipments |
| Packing list | Contents verification | All shipments |
| Bill of lading | Shipping contract | Sea freight |
| Certificate of origin | Trade agreement eligibility | Some destinations |
| Inspection report | Quality verification | Recommended |
| Export license | Legal compliance | Some products |

## Yiwu Sourcing Pricing Models

### Commission Model
The sourcing company charges a percentage of the shipment value. This is the most common pricing model.

| Commission Range | Typical Services Included |
|-----------------|--------------------------|
| 3–5% | Basic sourcing and communication |
| 5–8% | Sourcing, negotiation, basic QC |
| 8–10% | Full service: sourcing, QC, consolidation, shipping |

### Flat Service Fee
A fixed project fee is charged regardless of shipment value. This model is less common but can be more predictable for large orders.

### Hybrid Model
A smaller service fee plus reduced commission percentage. This model balances fixed costs with variable value.

### Pricing Comparison

| Order Value | Commission (5%) | Flat Fee ($500) | Hybrid ($200 + 3%) |
|------------|----------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| $2,000 | $100 | $500 | $260 |
| $5,000 | $250 | $500 | $350 |
| $10,000 | $500 | $500 | $500 |
| $20,000 | $1,000 | $500 | $800 |

## Common Mistakes Buyers Should Avoid

1. **Relying on only one supplier quotation**: Always compare at least 3 suppliers
2. **Skipping inspection before shipment**: Never skip quality verification
3. **Paying deposits to personal accounts**: Always use corporate bank accounts
4. **Assuming all suppliers are factories**: Verify supplier type and capability
5. **Ignoring packaging requirements early in specification**: Packaging mistakes are costly
6. **Not calculating total landed cost**: Product price is only part of the cost
7. **Rushing the process**: Sourcing takes time; rushing leads to mistakes
8. **Poor communication**: Vague specifications lead to quality issues
9. **Not keeping records**: Documentation protects you in disputes
10. **Choosing based on price alone**: The cheapest option is often the most expensive in the long run

## FAQ

### How long does the Yiwu sourcing process take?
Typical timeline: product brief (1–2 days), supplier vetting (3–5 days), sample approval (7–14 days), production (15–45 days), inspection (1–2 days), consolidation and shipping (3–7 days). Total: 30–75 days depending on product complexity.

### How much does a Yiwu sourcing service cost?
Most agents charge 5–10% commission on order value. For a $10,000 order, expect to pay $500–$1,000 in service fees. This typically includes sourcing, negotiation, basic quality control, and consolidation.

### Can I use multiple sourcing agents?
Yes, but it adds complexity. Each agent has their own network and processes. Using multiple agents can work if they specialize in different product categories, but be prepared to manage multiple relationships.

### What happens if my sourcing agent makes a mistake?
A professional agent should have a clear dispute resolution process. Reputable agents take responsibility for errors within their scope of work. Always confirm the agent's liability policy before engaging their services.

### Do I need to visit China to source from Yiwu?
No, a good sourcing agent can handle the entire process remotely. However, visiting China is recommended for building trust and understanding the market, especially for large or long-term sourcing relationships.

### How do I choose between commission and flat fee pricing?
For orders under $5,000, commission pricing is usually more cost-effective. For orders over $10,000, flat fee pricing may be more economical. Consider your order frequency and volume when choosing.

### Can I negotiate the sourcing agent's commission?
Yes, commission is often negotiable, especially for large orders or long-term relationships. However, be cautious of agents who agree to very low commissions — they may cut corners on service quality.

### What if the supplier delivers late?
A good sourcing agent will monitor production and identify delays early. If a supplier is going to miss the deadline, discuss options: partial shipment, expedited production, or alternative sourcing.

### How do I handle quality issues after receiving goods?
Document everything with photos and measurements. Contact your sourcing agent immediately with the inspection report. For issues within the agent's scope of work, they should take responsibility. For supplier issues, the agent can help negotiate resolution.

### Should I use a local or China-based sourcing agent?
China-based agents have direct market access and lower operational costs. Local agents may offer better time zone alignment and language support. For most importers, a China-based agent with good English communication is the best choice.
