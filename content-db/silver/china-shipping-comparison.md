## Section 1: Shipping Methods Overview

### Subsection 1.1: The Three Main Shipping Methods

When importing from China, you have three primary shipping methods to choose from. Each serves a different purpose and price point.

**1. Sea Freight (Ocean Shipping)**
The workhorse of international trade. Approximately 80% of global merchandise by volume moves by sea. For importers, this is the go-to method for large orders due to its low cost per unit.

**2. Air Freight**
The speed-cost balance. Air freight costs 4-8x more than sea freight but delivers in days instead of weeks. Ideal for medium-sized shipments where time-to-market matters.

**3. Express Courier**
The premium option. Services like DHL, FedEx, UPS, and SF Express offer door-to-door delivery in 3-5 business days. Best for samples, small orders, and urgent shipments.

### Subsection 1.2: Quick Decision Matrix

| Your Situation | Recommended Method | Why |
|---------------|-------------------|-----|
| First order, testing product | Express | Fast, low commitment |
| Reorder, 50-200 kg | Air freight | Balance of speed and cost |
| Bulk order, 500+ kg | Sea freight (FCL) | Lowest cost per unit |
| Urgent restock | Air freight or Express | Minimize stockout time |
| Heavy/bulky products | Sea freight only | Air becomes prohibitively expensive |
| Lightweight, high-value | Air freight | Cost is small % of product value |
| Seasonal products | Sea freight (plan ahead) | Budget-friendly with lead time |

### Subsection 1.3: Key Terms Every Importer Must Know

Before diving into each method, understand these essential shipping terms:

- **FOB (Free on Board):** Supplier delivers goods to the port; you pay for shipping from there
- **CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight):** Supplier pays for shipping to destination port
- **CBM (Cubic Meter):** Standard unit for measuring cargo volume
- **Volumetric Weight:** Calculated weight based on package dimensions (used by air/express)
- **Customs Clearance:** Process of clearing goods through customs at destination
- **Bill of Lading (B/L):** Document issued by carrier confirming receipt of goods
- **AWB (Air Waybill):** Air freight receipt and contract of carriage

### Subsection 2.1: FCL vs LCL

**Full Container Load (FCL)**
You rent an entire container for your cargo. Best for shipments exceeding 15 CBM or 10,000 kg.

|---------------|------------------------|----------|-------------|

| 40ft Standard | 12.03m x 2.35m x 2.39m | 67 CBM | 28,700 kg |

Your cargo shares container space with other shippers. You pay per CBM, making it ideal for smaller shipments under 15 CBM.

## Section 3: Air Freight Deep Dive

### Subsection 3.1: How Air Freight Pricing Works

Air freight pricing is based on **chargeable weight** — the greater of actual weight or volumetric weight.

**Volumetric Weight Formula:**
- For most carriers: Length (cm) x Width (cm) x Height (cm) / 6000
- Result in kilograms

**Example:** A box measuring 60cm x 40cm x 30cm = 72,000 / 6000 = 12 kg volumetric weight. If actual weight is 8 kg, you pay for 12 kg.

**When Sea Freight Becomes Cheaper Than Air:**

The break-even point between sea and air freight typically occurs at:

## Section 7: How to Choose the Right Method

### Subsection 7.1: Decision Framework

**Step 1: Calculate Your Cost per Unit**

```
Total Shipping Cost / Number of Units = Cost per Unit
```

Compare this to your product profit margin. If shipping cost exceeds 30% of product cost, reconsider your method.

**Step 2: Assess Time Sensitivity**

- **Can wait 4+ weeks:** Sea freight
- **Need in 1-2 weeks:** Air freight
- **Need in 3-5 days:** Express

**Step 3: Consider Product Characteristics**

| Product Type | Recommended Method | Reason |
|-------------|-------------------|--------|
| Electronics | Sea or Air | High value justifies air if urgent |
| Clothing | Sea | High volume, low weight, seasonal |
| Fragile items | Sea | Less handling |
| Perishables | Air or Express | Time critical |
| Heavy machinery | Sea only | Cost prohibitive by air |
| Samples | Express | Small quantities |

### Subsection 7.2: Industry-Specific Recommendations

**E-commerce Sellers:**
- **Restocking:** Air freight (5-10 day lead time)
- **Seasonal stock:** Sea freight (plan 6-8 weeks ahead)
- **New product testing:** Express (5-10 units)
- **Prime Day/Black Friday prep:** Sea freight (order 3 months early)

**Amazon FBA Sellers:**
- **First shipment:** Air freight (fast, test demand)
- **Replenishment:** Sea freight (cost-effective)
- **Urgent restock:** Air freight or express
- **Q4 prep:** Sea freight by August

**Brick-and-Mortar Retailers:**
- **Regular inventory:** Sea freight (predictable, low cost)
- **Emergency orders:** Air freight
- **New store openings:** Mix of sea and air

## Section 8: Hybrid Shipping Strategies

### Subsection 8.1: The Split Shipment Strategy

Many successful importers use a hybrid approach:

**Strategy 1: Sea + Air Combination**
- Ship 70% by sea (cost-effective)
- Ship 30% by air (fast replenishment)
- Result: Lower average cost with faster average delivery

**Strategy 2: Sample → Air → Sea Progression**
- Sample: Express (5-10 units, 3-5 days)
- First order: Air freight (100-200 units, 7-10 days)
- Reorders: Sea freight (500+ units, 20-30 days)

**Strategy 3: Inventory Positioning**
- Ship bulk by sea to regional warehouse
- Use air freight for fast distribution to sales channels
- Maintain safety stock at destination

### Subsection 8.2: Cost Optimization Tips

1. **Consolidate shipments** when possible to reach FCL minimums
2. **Negotiate rates** with freight forwarders for volume discounts
3. **Book early** during peak seasons (August-October for Q4)
4. **Use LCL** for orders under 15 CBM to avoid paying for empty space
5. **Consider rail freight** for Europe (China-Europe Railway Express, 15-20 days)

## Section 9: Hidden Costs and Pitfalls

### Subsection 9.1: Common Hidden Costs

**Port Charges:**
- Terminal handling charges (THC): $100-300 per container
- Documentation fees: $50-100
- Bill of lading fees: $30-50

**Customs Charges:**
- Customs broker fees: $150-300
- Duty deposits (varies by country)
- VAT/GST (varies by country)

**Inland Transportation:**
- Trucking from port to warehouse: $200-800 (domestic)
- Warehouse handling: $0.50-2.00 per unit

**Insurance:**
- Marine cargo insurance: 0.3-0.5% of cargo value
- Air freight insurance: 0.1-0.3% of cargo value

### Subsection 9.2: Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Underestimating total cost** — Always add 15-20% buffer
2. **Poor packaging** — Damaged goods cost more than proper packaging
3. **Incorrect documentation** — Can cause delays of weeks
4. **Ignoring Incoterms** — FOB vs CIF affects who pays what
5. **Not insuring cargo** — One lost container can bankrupt a small business

## Case Study

### Small Business Success: LED Strip Importer

**Company:** LED Solutions LLC, based in Austin, Texas
**Product:** LED strip lights from Shenzhen
**Challenge:** Balancing cost and speed for a growing Amazon FBA business

**The Problem:**
LED Solutions was shipping 100% of their inventory by air freight at $6.50/kg. While this provided fast delivery (7-10 days), it was eating into their margins significantly. At 500 kg per month, they were spending $3,250/month on shipping alone.

**The Solution:**
Working with a freight forwarder, they implemented a hybrid strategy:

1. **80% by sea freight** (400 kg/month at $1.20/kg = $480)

3. **Maintained 2-week safety stock** to cover sea freight transit time

**Results:**

**Key Takeaway:** By accepting a slightly longer average delivery time and maintaining strategic safety stock, LED Solutions reduced shipping costs by 65% while improving inventory stability.

## FAQ

### 1. What is the cheapest way to ship from China?

Sea freight is the cheapest method, especially FCL (Full Container Load) for orders over 15 CBM. LCL is economical for smaller shipments. For reference, sea freight costs $0.50-3.00 per kg compared to $4-8 for air and $8-25 for express.

### 2. How long does shipping from China take?

Transit times vary by method: Express takes 3-5 days, air freight takes 5-10 days, and sea freight takes 15-45 days depending on destination. Add 3-7 days for customs clearance and inland transportation.

### 3. Can I track my shipment from China?

Yes. All major carriers offer tracking. Sea freight tracking provides port-to-port updates. Air freight offers real-time tracking. Express couriers provide detailed door-to-door tracking with delivery notifications.

### 4. What documents do I need for shipping from China?

Essential documents include: Commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading (sea) or air waybill (air), certificate of origin, and product compliance certificates (CE, FDA, etc.). Your freight forwarder can help prepare these.

### 5. How do I calculate shipping costs from China?

Use this formula:
- **Sea LCL:** CBM x rate per CBM (or minimum charge)
- **Sea FCL:** Fixed container rate + surcharges + fees
- **Air freight:** Chargeable weight x rate per kg (max of actual or volumetric weight)
- **Express:** Package weight/dimensions x carrier rate

### 6. What are the risks of sea freight?

Main risks include: delays from port congestion, potential cargo damage from rough seas, longer transit times affecting cash flow, and vulnerability to weather disruptions. Marine cargo insurance mitigates financial risk.

### 7. Should I use a freight forwarder or book directly?

For most importers, a freight forwarder is recommended. They provide: consolidated rates, documentation assistance, customs clearance, door-to-door service, and expertise in handling problems. Direct booking is only cost-effective for very large, experienced importers.

### 8. What happens if my shipment is delayed?

Contact your freight forwarder immediately. Common delay causes include: port congestion (wait for berth), customs inspection (provide requested documents), or weather (wait for clearance). Freight forwarders can sometimes expedite by switching carriers or routes.

### 9. How do I choose between FCL and LCL?

Choose FCL if your cargo exceeds 15 CBM or weighs over 10,000 kg. LCL is better for smaller shipments. Calculate the break-even point: if LCL cost approaches 80% of FCL cost, FCL becomes more economical even if you don't fill the container completely.

### 10. What are Incoterms and why do they matter?

Incoterms (International Commercial Terms) define who is responsible for shipping costs, insurance, and risk at each stage. Common terms include FOB (you pay from Chinese port), CIF (supplier pays to destination port), and DDP (supplier pays everything including import duties). Understanding Incoterms prevents surprise costs.
