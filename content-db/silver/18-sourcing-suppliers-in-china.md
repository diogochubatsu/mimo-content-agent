## How to Verify Suppliers

### Check Business Licenses
Always request a Chinese business license before placing orders.

**Business License Verification Steps**:
1. Request high-resolution photo of license
2. Verify on National Enterprise Credit Information system (gsxt.gov.cn)
3. Confirm company name matches all documents
4. Check business scope includes manufacturing/trading
5. Verify registered address is legitimate
6. Confirm legal representative name

### Request Product Samples
Samples help buyers evaluate product quality and supplier reliability.

**Sample Best Practices**:
- Order from 2–3 suppliers for comparison
- Pay for samples (free samples may not represent quality)
- Document specifications (photos, measurements)
- Test thoroughly (function, durability, packaging)
- Keep approved samples for future reference

### Inspect Supplier Communication
Reliable suppliers usually reply clearly and consistently.

**Communication Evaluation**:
| Factor | Excellent | Good | Poor |
|--------|----------|------|------|
| Response time | Within 4 hours | Within 24 hours | 2+ days |
| Language quality | Fluent English | Basic English | Translation needed |
| Detail level | Comprehensive | Adequate | Vague |
| Proactive updates | Regular | On request | Never |

### Use Third-Party Inspection Services
Before shipping products, buyers should arrange inspections.

**Inspection Types**:
- Desktop verification (online only)
- Video audit (remote inspection)
- On-site audit (physical visit)
- Pre-shipment inspection (before shipping)

## How to Negotiate With Chinese Suppliers

### Request Multiple Quotations
Get quotes from at least 3 suppliers for the same product. This gives you comparison points and negotiating leverage.

### Clarify Specifications Clearly
Vague specifications lead to misunderstandings. Provide detailed product requirements including dimensions, materials, colors, packaging, and quantities.

### Build Long-Term Relationships
Chinese suppliers value long-term relationships. Consistent orders, clear communication, and fair treatment lead to better pricing and service over time.

### Negotiation Strategies

| Strategy | When to Use | Expected Outcome |
|----------|------------|-----------------|
| Volume commitment | Large orders | 5–15% price reduction |
| Payment upfront | First orders | 3–5% discount |
| Long-term contract | Established relationship | Better pricing and priority |
| Multiple product order | Diverse needs | Bundle discount |
| Off-season ordering | Non-peak times | 10–20% savings |

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Choosing based on price alone**: The cheapest supplier often has hidden quality issues
2. **Skipping verification**: Always verify business licenses and bank accounts
3. **Ignoring samples**: Never order bulk without testing samples first
4. **Poor communication**: Vague specifications lead to quality problems
5. **No inspection**: Always arrange pre-shipment inspection
6. **Rushing decisions**: Take time to compare and evaluate suppliers
7. **Paying to personal accounts**: Always use corporate bank accounts
8. **Not researching market trends**: Understand what products are in demand before sourcing

## FAQ

### What is the best way to find suppliers in China?
The best method depends on your experience, order size, and product type. For beginners, B2B platforms like Alibaba provide buyer protection. For experienced importers, wholesale markets and trade fairs offer better pricing and variety.

### Do I need to visit China to find suppliers?
No, but visiting is highly recommended. In-person visits allow you to verify suppliers, inspect quality, negotiate better terms, and build relationships. For remote sourcing, use a reputable sourcing agent.

### How do I verify a Chinese supplier's business license?
Use the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (www.gsxt.gov.cn) to verify the license. Enter the company name or unified social credit code to check registration status and details.

### What are the risks of sourcing from China?
Main risks include supplier fraud, quality inconsistencies, communication barriers, and shipping delays. These risks are mitigated by proper verification, sample testing, inspection, and working with reputable agents.

### How much does a sourcing agent cost?
Most agents charge 5–10% commission on order value. For a $10,000 order, expect to pay $500–$1,000 in agent fees. This typically includes sourcing, negotiation, basic QC, and consolidation.

### Which Chinese city is best for sourcing?
It depends on your product. Yiwu for small commodities, Shenzhen for electronics, Guangzhou for fashion and leather goods. Many importers combine sourcing from multiple cities.

### Can I trust suppliers I find on Alibaba?
Alibaba provides Trade Assurance protection, but this does not guarantee product quality. Always verify suppliers, order samples, and arrange inspection regardless of platform.

### How long does the sourcing process take?
Typical timeline: research (1–2 weeks), supplier comparison (1–2 weeks), sample approval (1–2 weeks), production (2–6 weeks), shipping (2–6 weeks). Total: 2–4 months from start to delivery.

### What is the minimum order quantity for Chinese suppliers?
MOQ varies widely. Factory orders typically require 100–1,000+ units. Yiwu market suppliers often accept 1–5 cartons. Alibaba suppliers range from 1–500 units depending on the product.

### How do I protect my intellectual property in China?
Register trademarks in China before manufacturing. Use non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) with suppliers. Work with reputable agents who understand IP protection. Consider using escrow payment for first orders.
