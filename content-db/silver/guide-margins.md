## Section 1: Understanding Import Margins

### 1.1 What is an Import Margin?

**Import Margin Formula:**
```
Margin = (Selling Price - Total Landed Cost) / Selling Price × 100
```

**Example:**
- Selling Price: $24.99
- Total Landed Cost: $12.00
- Margin: ($24.99 - $12.00) / $24.99 × 100 = **52%**

### 1.2 Why Margins Matter

**Margin Impact on Business:**

| Margin | Profit per $10K Sales | Sustainability |
|--------|----------------------|----------------|
| 20% | $2,000 | Barely viable |
| 30% | $3,000 |勉强维持 |
| 40% | $4,000 | Good |
| 50% | $5,000 | Excellent |
| 60%+ | $6,000+ | Outstanding |

### 1.3 Common Margin Mistakes

**Mistake #1: Forgetting Platform Fees**
- Amazon FBA: 30-40% of selling price
- Mercado Livre: 16-20% of selling price
- eBay: 13-15% of selling price

**Mistake #2: Underestimating Shipping**
- Air freight: $4-8/kg
- Sea freight: $0.50-2/kg
- Express: $10-20/kg

**Mistake #3: Ignoring Customs Duties**
- US: 0-25% depending on product
- EU: 0-20% + VAT
- UK: 0-20% + VAT

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Research Product Costs

1. Get quotes from 3+ suppliers
2. Include customization costs
3. Calculate sample costs (amortized)
4. Document all costs in spreadsheet

### Step 2: Calculate Shipping Costs

1. Get freight quotes (air and sea)
2. Include all surcharges
3. Calculate per-unit shipping cost
4. Consider shipping time vs cost tradeoff

### Step 3: Determine Customs Duties

1. Find HS code for product
2. Check duty rate for your country
3. Calculate customs value (product + shipping)
4. Add any additional fees (handling, inspection)

### Step 4: Add Platform Fees

1. Research platform fee structure
2. Include referral fees, FBA fees, storage
3. Calculate total platform costs
4. Factor in payment processing fees

### Step 5: Include Other Costs

1. Budget for returns (5-8%)
2. Budget for defects (2-3%)
3. Include photography and listing costs
4. Add PPC advertising budget (10-20%)

### Step 6: Calculate Final Margin

1. Sum all costs (landed cost)
2. Subtract from selling price
3. Calculate margin percentage
4. Verify against 40% minimum target

## FAQ

**Q: What's a good import margin?**
A: Target 40%+ after all costs. This provides buffer for unexpected expenses and profitable growth. Below 30% is risky; above 60% is excellent.

**Q: How do I calculate customs duties?**
A: 1) Find HS code for your product, 2) Check duty rate for your country, 3) Calculate customs value (product + shipping), 4) Multiply by duty rate. Use online calculators for accuracy.

**Q: What are the most underestimated costs?**
A: Platform fees (30-40%), returns (5-8%), PPC advertising (10-20%), and defects (2-3%). Most new importers only consider product and shipping costs.

**Q: How do I improve my margins?**
A: 1) Negotiate better prices, 2) Optimize shipping (sea vs air), 3) Reduce returns (better photos/descriptions), 4) Bundle products, 5) Build brand premium.

**Q: Should I include my time in margin calculations?**
A: Yes, especially if you're paying yourself a salary. Include hourly rate × time spent on the product. This gives true profitability.

**Q: How often should I recalculate margins?**
A: Monthly at minimum. Costs change: shipping rates, platform fees, currency fluctuations. Review and adjust pricing accordingly.

**Q: What's the difference between gross and net margin?**
A: Gross margin = (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue. Net margin includes all expenses (platform fees, marketing, overhead). Always use net margin for profitability analysis.
