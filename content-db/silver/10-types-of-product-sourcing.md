# Types of Product Sourcing: Complete Guide to Sourcing Models and Strategies

Understanding the different types of product sourcing is crucial for building a profitable import business. Each model has unique advantages, risks, and cost structures.

This complete guide covers all major sourcing models including direct factory sourcing, trading companies, sourcing agents, dropshipping, wholesale, private label, and OEM/ODM manufacturing. Based on real data from successful importers.

## Sourcing Models Overview

### Comparison Matrix

| Model | Cost | Control | Risk | MOQ | Best For |
|-------|------|---------|------|-----|----------|
| Direct factory | Lowest | High | Medium | High | High volume |
| Trading company | Medium | Medium | Low | Low | Small orders |
| Sourcing agent | Higher | High | Low | Varies | Complex orders |
| Dropshipping | Medium | Low | Low | None | Testing |
| Wholesale | Medium | Low | Low | Low | Quick start |
| Private label | Higher | Highest | Medium | Medium | Brand building |
| OEM | Higher | Highest | Medium | High | Custom products |
| ODM | Medium | High | Low | Medium | Fast launch |

## Model Deep Dives

### 1. Direct Factory Sourcing

**Definition:** Buying directly from manufacturers without intermediaries.

**Advantages:**
- Lowest unit cost
- Full customization control
- Direct communication
- Build long-term relationships

**Disadvantages:**
- Higher MOQs required
- Requires sourcing expertise
- More time-consuming
- Higher risk for beginners

**Best For:**
- High-volume importers
- Experienced buyers
- Custom product requirements
- Long-term partnerships

**Cost Structure:**

| Cost Component | Percentage |
|----------------|------------|
| Product cost | 60-70% |
| Freight | 10-15% |
| Duty | 5-10% |
| Other fees | 5-10% |

### 2. Trading Companies

**Definition:** Intermediaries that source from multiple factories.

**Advantages:**
- Lower MOQs
- Multiple factory options
- Quality verification
- Export documentation

**Disadvantages:**
- Higher unit cost (10-30% markup)
- Less direct control
- Potential communication gaps
- Limited customization

**Best For:**
- Small to medium orders
- First-time importers
- Multi-SKU orders
- Quick turnaround

**Cost Structure:**

| Cost Component | Percentage |
|----------------|------------|
| Product cost | 55-65% |
| Trading company margin | 10-30% |
| Freight | 8-12% |
| Duty | 5-10% |
| Other fees | 5-10% |

### 3. Sourcing Agents

**Definition:** Professional intermediaries that manage the entire sourcing process.

**Advantages:**
- Expert negotiation
- Factory verification
- Quality control
- Language/culture bridge

**Disadvantages:**
- Agent fees (5-15%)
- Communication layer
- Potential conflicts of interest
- Variable quality

**Best For:**
- Complex product requirements
- First-time importers
- High-value orders
- Compliance-heavy products

**Cost Structure:**

| Cost Component | Percentage |
|----------------|------------|
| Product cost | 55-65% |
| Agent fee | 5-15% |
| Freight | 8-12% |
| Duty | 5-10% |
| Other fees | 5-10% |

### 4. Dropshipping

**Definition:** Supplier ships directly to customer, no inventory holding.

**Advantages:**
- No inventory risk
- Low startup cost
- Test products easily
- Scalable

**Disadvantages:**
- Lower margins (20-35%)
- Less quality control
- Longer shipping times
- Customer service challenges

**Best For:**
- Product testing
- Low capital start
- Market validation
- Passive income

**Cost Structure:**

| Cost Component | Percentage |
|----------------|------------|
| Product cost | 40-50% |
| Supplier margin | 15-25% |
| Shipping | 10-20% |
| Platform fees | 5-10% |

### 5. Wholesale Sourcing

**Definition:** Buying pre-made products in bulk from distributors.

**Advantages:**
- Quick to market
- No customization needed
- Established products
- Lower risk

**Disadvantages:**
- Higher unit cost
- Less differentiation
- Competition from other sellers
- Limited branding

**Best For:**
- Quick revenue generation
- Established product categories
- Limited sourcing expertise
- Test before private label

**Cost Structure:**

| Cost Component | Percentage |
|----------------|------------|
| Product cost | 50-60% |
| Distributor margin | 15-25% |
| Shipping | 5-10% |
| Other fees | 5-10% |

### 6. Private Label

**Definition:** Branding existing products with your own label.

**Advantages:**
- Brand ownership
- Higher margins (50-70%)
- Less competition
- Customer loyalty

**Disadvantages:**
- Higher upfront cost
- Longer time to market
- IP protection needed
- Marketing investment

**Best For:**
- Brand building
- Long-term business
- Amazon FBA
- E-commerce

**Cost Structure:**

| Cost Component | Percentage |
|----------------|------------|
| Product cost | 55-65% |
| Branding/packaging | 5-10% |
| Freight | 8-12% |
| Duty | 5-10% |
| Other fees | 5-10% |

### 7. OEM Manufacturing

**Definition:** Manufacturer builds your custom design.

**Advantages:**
- Full customization
- IP ownership
- Unique products
- Higher margins

**Disadvantages:**
- Higher MOQs
- Tooling costs
- Longer lead times
- More risk

**Best For:**
- Unique product ideas
- Brand differentiation
- High-volume products
- Long-term portfolio

**Cost Structure:**

| Cost Component | Percentage |
|----------------|------------|
| Tooling/molds | 10-20% (amortized) |
| Product cost | 55-65% |
| Freight | 8-12% |
| Duty | 5-10% |
| Other fees | 5-10% |

### 8. ODM Manufacturing

**Definition:** Manufacturer provides design, you brand it.

**Advantages:**
- Fast time to market
- No tooling cost
- Lower MOQs
- Lower risk

**Disadvantages:**
- Less customization
- Supplier owns design
- More competition
- Limited differentiation

**Best For:**
- Fast launch
- Limited budget
- Market testing
- Standard products

**Cost Structure:**

| Cost Component | Percentage |
|----------------|------------|
| Product cost | 60-70% |
| Branding | 3-5% |
| Freight | 8-12% |
| Duty | 5-10% |
| Other fees | 5-10% |

## Model Selection Decision Tree

```
What's your primary goal?
├── Lowest cost
│   ├── High volume → Direct factory
│   └── Low volume → Trading company
├── Brand building
│   ├── Custom design → OEM
│   └── Standard product → Private label/ODM
├── Fast launch
│   ├── No inventory → Dropshipping
│   └── Inventory → Wholesale/ODM
└── Complex requirements
    └── Sourcing agent
```

## People Also Ask

1. **What's the best sourcing model for beginners?** Trading company or wholesale for low risk. See our [How to Import From China](/how-to-import-from-china/) guide.

2. **What's the difference between OEM and ODM?** OEM: You design, manufacturer builds. ODM: Manufacturer designs, you brand. See our [OEM vs ODM Manufacturing](/oem-vs-odm-manufacturing/) guide.

3. **When should I use a sourcing agent?** Complex products, high-value orders, or first-time importers. See our [Best China Sourcing Agents](/best-china-sourcing-agents/) comparison.

4. **What's the lowest MOQ model?** Dropshipping (no MOQ) or wholesale (100+ units).

5. **How do I choose between models?** Consider: budget, experience, product complexity, volume, and timeline.

6. **Can I switch models later?** Yes, many sellers start with wholesale and transition to private label.

7. **What's the riskiest model?** OEM (highest upfront investment) or dropshipping (lowest quality control).

8. **How do I verify suppliers in any model?** Check business license on gsxt.gov.cn, request samples, verify references. See our [How to Verify Suppliers](/how-to-verify-suppliers/) guide.

9. **What's the most profitable model?** Private label/OEM at scale (50-70% margins).

10. **How do I start with minimal capital?** Dropshipping or wholesale with small test orders.
