## Section 1: The Three Core Margin Formulas

### 1.1 Gross Margin Formula

**The Basics:**
```
Gross Margin = (Selling Price - Product Cost) / Selling Price × 100
```

**Why It's Dangerous:** This formula only considers the product cost from your supplier. It ignores shipping, duties, platform fees, returns, and every other cost that actually matters. Most "profitable" products on 1688 fail this test once you add real costs.

**Example:**
- Product cost: $3.50 (LED strips from 1688)
- Selling price: $24.99
- Gross margin: ($24.99 - $3.50) / $24.99 × 100 = **86%**

This looks amazing. But it's a lie.

### 1.2 Net Margin Formula (The One That Matters)

**The Real Formula:**
```
Net Margin = (Selling Price - Total Landed Cost) / Selling Price × 100
```

**Total Landed Cost = Product Cost + Shipping + Customs + Platform Fees + Other Costs**

**Same Example, Real Numbers:**
- Product cost: $3.50
- Shipping (air freight): $4.20
- Customs duties (6%): $0.46
- Amazon FBA fees: $7.75
- Returns budget (6%): $1.50
- PPC advertising (12%): $3.00
- Defects budget (3%): $0.11
- **Total Landed Cost: $20.52**
- **Net Margin: ($24.99 - $20.52) / $24.99 × 100 = 17.9%**

From 86% to 17.9%. That's the reality.

### 1.3 ROI (Return on Investment) Formula

**For Comparing Opportunities:**
```
ROI = (Net Profit per Unit × Units Sold) / Total Investment × 100
```

**Example:**
- Net profit per unit: $4.47 ($24.99 - $20.52)
- Units ordered: 500
- Total investment: $10,260 (500 × $20.52)
- ROI: ($4.47 × 500) / $10,260 × 100 = **21.8%**

**ROI Comparison Table:**

| Product | Investment | Annual Profit | ROI | Verdict |
|---------|-----------|---------------|-----|---------|
| LED Strips | $10,260 | $2,235 | 21.8% | Moderate |
| Phone Cases | $2,210 | $1,490 | 67.4% | Excellent |
| Bluetooth Earbuds | $7,055 | $1,340 | 19.0% | Low |
| Resistance Bands | $3,030 | $1,485 | 49.0% | Good |
| Kitchen Gadgets | $3,285 | $1,560 | 47.5% | Good |

### 1.4 Break-Even Formula

**How Many Units to Cover Fixed Costs:**
```
Break-Even Units = Fixed Costs / (Selling Price - Variable Cost per Unit)
```

**Example:**
- Fixed costs: $1,500 (samples, photography, listing setup, initial PPC)
- Selling price: $24.99
- Variable cost per unit: $20.52
- Contribution margin: $4.47
- **Break-Even: 336 units**

If you order 500 units and sell 336, you've covered your fixed costs. The remaining 164 units generate pure profit.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Gather Your Product Data

1. Get the exact unit price from your supplier (not the sample price)
2. Add customization costs (logo, packaging, labels)
3. Divide sample cost by expected order quantity
4. **Document everything in a spreadsheet**

### Step 2: Calculate Shipping Costs

1. Get quotes for at least 2 shipping methods (air and sea)
2. Include ALL surcharges (fuel, security, handling)
3. Calculate per-unit shipping cost
4. Add insurance (0.5-1% of product value)
5. **Factor in lead time impact on cash flow**

### Step 3: Determine Customs and Duties

1. Find the HS code for your product (use hsbianma.com or customs.gov)
2. Check duty rate for your destination country
3. Calculate customs value: product cost + shipping + insurance
4. Add any additional import taxes (VAT, GST, ICMS)
5. **Include customs broker fees if applicable ($100-300)**

### Step 4: Add Platform Fees

1. Look up current fee structure for your selling platform
2. Include referral fees, fulfillment fees, storage fees
3. Calculate payment processing fees
4. Add monthly subscription costs (divided by expected monthly sales)
5. **Platform fees are often 30-40% of selling price**

### Step 5: Budget for Variable Costs

1. Returns: budget 5-8% of selling price
2. Defects: budget 2-4% of product cost
3. PPC advertising: budget 10-20% of selling price
4. Customer service: budget 1-2% of revenue
5. Photography/listing: divide total cost by expected units sold

### Step 6: Calculate and Validate

1. Sum ALL costs to get total landed cost
2. Calculate: (Selling Price - Landed Cost) / Selling Price × 100
3. Compare against minimum margin target (35%)
4. Run sensitivity analysis (best/base/worst case)
5. **If margin < 35%, find ways to reduce costs or increase price**

## FAQ

**Q: What's the difference between gross margin and net margin?**
A: Gross margin only considers product cost (Selling Price - Product Cost) / Selling Price. Net margin includes ALL costs: shipping, duties, platform fees, returns, advertising, and overhead. Always use net margin for import business decisions — gross margin is misleading.

**Q: What is a good import margin for Amazon FBA?**
A: Target 35-45% net margin for Amazon FBA products. Below 30% leaves too little room for unexpected costs and price competition. Above 50% is excellent but rare for competitive categories.

**Q: How do I calculate customs duties for my product?**
A: 1) Find your product's HS code at hsbianma.com or your country's customs website. 2) Look up the duty rate for that HS code. 3) Calculate customs value (product cost + shipping + insurance). 4) Multiply customs value by duty rate. For example: $500 customs value × 5% duty = $25 duty.

**Q: Should I include PPC advertising costs in my margin calculation?**
A: Absolutely. PPC is not optional for most Amazon and Shopify sellers. Budget 10-20% of your selling price for advertising. Many new importers forget this cost and end up with negative margins despite strong sales.

**Q: How do currency fluctuations affect my margins?**
A: Currency fluctuations of 1-3% are common month-to-month. This can impact your margins by 1-3 percentage points. Consider this when calculating long-term profitability, and price your products with a buffer for FX changes.

**Q: What's the minimum order quantity impact on margins?**
A: Smaller orders have higher per-unit costs for shipping, samples, and setup. Ordering 100 units might cost $4.26/unit for shipping, while 1,000 units cost $2.20/unit. Scale up as quickly as your cash flow allows to improve margins.

**Q: How do I account for product returns in margin calculations?**
A: Budget 5-8% of your selling price for returns. This varies by category: clothing has 15-25% returns, electronics 5-10%, home goods 3-7%. Track your actual return rate over time and adjust your calculations accordingly.

**Q: Can I use the same margin formula for all selling platforms?**
A: The formula is the same, but the cost components differ. Amazon FBA has higher fees (30-40%) but includes fulfillment. Shopify has lower fees (3-5%) but requires your own fulfillment. Mercado Livre has different commission structures. Adjust the platform fee line in your calculation for each platform.

**Q: How often should I recalculate margins for existing products?**
A: Monthly at minimum. Shipping rates, platform fees, duty rates, and currency exchange rates all change. Review your margins monthly and adjust pricing if costs increase. A product that was profitable at 40% margin can become a loss at 20% if costs rise unexpectedly.
