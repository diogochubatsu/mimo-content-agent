A useful Yiwu inspection report should identify the inspected goods, explain the sampling method, record quantity and product checks, classify every defect, and provide clear photographs or measurements. It should also state what was not checked. Without a proper inspection report, you are essentially gambling with your order. This guide explains every section of a professional Yiwu inspection report and how to use it to protect your investment.

## Why Yiwu Inspection Reports Matter

When sourcing from Yiwu Market, you are often dealing with dozens of suppliers, mixed product categories, and varying quality levels. A proper inspection report serves as your quality shield before goods leave China. Without one, you risk receiving defective products, incorrect quantities, or items that do not match your specifications — all problems that become exponentially more expensive to resolve after international shipping.

### The True Cost of Skipping Inspection

Importers who skip pre-shipment inspection often face:
- **Return shipping costs**: $2,000–$10,000 to ship defective goods back to China
- **Replacement delays**: 30–60 days to produce and ship replacement products
- **Lost sales**: Amazon sellers may lose ranking and reviews due to quality complaints
- **Customer refunds**: 15–25% of orders may require refunds on defective products
- **Reputation damage**: Negative reviews and reduced seller ratings

## Start With Inspection Identity and Scope

The first page should make the assignment traceable. It should record the buyer, supplier, factory or warehouse address, purchase-order number, product names, SKU codes, inspection date, inspector and report number.

### Essential Identity Fields

| Field | Why It Matters |
|-------|---------------|
| Buyer name and contact | Establishes who requested the inspection |
| Supplier/company name | Identifies the responsible party |
| Factory or warehouse address | Confirms the physical location of goods |
| Purchase order number | Links inspection to your specific order |
| Product names and SKU codes | Ensures correct products are being inspected |
| Inspection date | Documents when the inspection occurred |
| Inspector name and ID | Provides accountability for findings |
| Report number | Creates a unique reference for future disputes |

## Show Order Status and Available Quantity

A Yiwu inspection report should compare ordered, finished, packed, presented and inspected quantities. This comparison reveals whether the supplier has produced enough goods and whether they are ready for shipment.

### Quantity Comparison Table

| Quantity Field | What It Tells the Buyer |
|----------------|------------------------|
| Ordered quantity | The contractual quantity by SKU or variation |
| Finished quantity | Units reported complete at inspection time |
| Packed quantity | Units already in retail or export packaging |
| Presented quantity | Goods actually made available to the inspector |
| Sample size | Units randomly selected and checked |

### What to Do When Quantities Don't Match

If finished quantity is significantly less than ordered quantity, you have several options:
1. **Delay shipment** until the supplier completes production
2. **Accept partial shipment** with a price adjustment
3. **Negotiate compensation** for the shortfall
4. **Cancel the remaining balance** if the supplier cannot fulfill

If presented quantity is less than finished quantity, the supplier may be hiding defective goods or diverting products to other orders. This is a red flag that requires immediate investigation.

## Explain the Sampling and Acceptance Rules

The report should name the sampling standard or buyer-approved method, lot size, inspection level, sample size and acceptance limits for critical, major and minor defects.

### Understanding AQL Sampling

Most professional inspections use the ANSI/ASQ Z1.4 (ISO 2859-1) sampling standard. Here is how it works:

| Lot Size | Sample Size Code | General Inspection Level II |
|----------|-----------------|---------------------------|
| 2–8 | A | 2 |
| 9–15 | B | 3 |
| 16–25 | C | 5 |
| 26–50 | D | 8 |
| 51–90 | E | 13 |
| 91–150 | F | 20 |
| 151–280 | G | 32 |
| 281–500 | H | 50 |
| 501–1,200 | J | 80 |
| 1,201–3,000 | K | 125 |
| 3,001–10,000 | L | 200 |
| 10,001–35,000 | M | 315 |

### Common AQL Acceptance Limits

| Defect Type | Typical AQL Limit | Meaning |
|-------------|-------------------|---------|
| Critical | 0 | Zero tolerance for safety/regulatory defects |
| Major | 2.5 | Up to 2.5% defective rate allowed |
| Minor | 4.0 | Up to 4.0% defective rate allowed |

**Important**: You should define your AQL limits BEFORE inspection begins. These limits should be agreed upon with your supplier and documented in your purchase contract.

## Include Product, Function and Workmanship Results

### Product Identity and Measurements
Confirm model, material, colour, dimensions, weight, components and accessories. The inspector should compare actual measurements against your approved specifications and record any deviations.

### Function and Assembly
Describe each functional test, sample size and outcome. For electronics, this includes power-on tests, button functions, connectivity checks, and battery life verification. For non-electronic products, this includes assembly tests, hinge durability, lid fit, and moving part functionality.

### Workmanship and Appearance
Record scratches, stains, loose parts, poor seams, sharp edges, deformation, colour differences and other relevant faults. The inspector should photograph each defect with both a close-up view and a context view showing the defect's location on the product.

### Inspection Checklist by Product Type

| Product Type | Key Checks |
|-------------|------------|
| Electronics | Power test, button function, LED indicator, battery compartment, cord length, plug type |
| Textiles | Fabric weight, stitching quality, print alignment, color consistency, label accuracy |
| Toys | Small parts test, sharp edge check, material smell, paint adhesion, packaging safety |
| Kitchenware | Handle strength, lid fit, material thickness, heat resistance, dishwasher compatibility |
| Jewelry | Clasp function, plating quality, stone setting, weight consistency, allergy testing |
| Bags | Zipper function, stitching strength, material texture, buckle quality, pocket alignment |

## Document Every Defect With Usable Evidence

For each defect type, the report should provide a description, classification, affected sample number, count and photographs showing both detail and product context.

### Defect Documentation Requirements

Each defect entry in the report should include:
1. **Defect ID**: Unique identifier for tracking
2. **Description**: Clear, factual description of the defect
3. **Classification**: Critical, Major, or Minor
4. **Sample number**: Which specific sample(s) are affected
5. **Count**: Total number of defective units found
6. **Photos**: At least 2 photos per defect (close-up + context)
7. **Measurement**: Quantitative data where applicable (e.g., scratch length, color deviation)

### Common Defect Examples by Category

| Product Category | Common Defects | Typical Classification |
|-----------------|----------------|----------------------|
| Electronics | Won't power on, wrong voltage, loose ports | Critical/Major |
| Textiles | Stains, loose threads, wrong size, print misalignment | Major/Minor |
| Toys | Sharp edges, small parts, broken mechanisms | Critical/Major |
| Kitchenware | Dents, rust spots, weak handles, wrong color | Major/Minor |
| Bags | Broken zippers, torn lining, crooked straps | Major/Minor |

## Verify Packaging, Labels and Shipping Marks

A Yiwu inspection report should cover retail packaging, inner packs, master cartons, carton dimensions, gross weight, closures, protection and shipping marks.

### Packaging Inspection Checklist

- ☐ Retail packaging matches approved design
- ☐ Inner packs are correctly labeled
- ☐ Master cartons are strong enough for international shipping
- ☐ Carton dimensions match specifications
- ☐ Gross weight is within acceptable range
- ☐ Shipping marks are correct and legible
- ☐ Barcodes scan correctly
- ☐ Country of origin labels are present
- ☐ Warning labels are present (for applicable products)
- ☐ Packing list matches physical contents

### Packaging Common Issues

| Issue | Risk Level | Prevention |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| Wrong barcode | High | Verify barcode during sample approval |
| Missing warning labels | Critical | Include label checklist in product specifications |
| Weak cartons | High | Specify carton grade and burst strength |
| Incorrect quantity per carton | Medium | Confirm packing quantity in advance |
| Damaged retail packaging | Medium | Request packaging samples before production |

## How to Review a Yiwu Inspection Report

1. Confirm the supplier, location, order number, SKUs and inspection date
2. Check whether enough goods were complete and available for random sampling
3. Compare the sample size and defect limits with the approved plan
4. Review critical findings before looking at the overall result
5. Match defect totals to photographs, sample numbers and classifications
6. Verify functions, measurements, packaging, labels and carton results
7. Identify restrictions, missing evidence and tests not performed
8. Decide whether to pass, hold, correct, reinspect or reject the lot

### Decision Framework After Reviewing

| Scenario | Recommended Action |
|----------|-------------------|
| All defects within AQL limits, no critical defects | Pass — proceed with shipment |
| Critical defect found, even if within count limit | Fail — reject and require correction |
| Major defects exceed AQL limit | Hold — discuss correction with supplier |
| Incomplete data or missing photos | Hold — request complete report |
| Quantities significantly below order | Hold — require completion before shipment |
| Packaging issues identified | Hold — require repacking |

## Case Study: How a Proper Inspection Report Saved $15,000

An Australian importer ordered 10,000 LED light strips from a Yiwu supplier. The pre-shipment inspection report revealed:
- **Critical defects**: 3 samples had exposed wiring (AQL limit: 0)
- **Major defects**: 8 samples had incorrect color temperature (AQL limit: 2.5, found: 8.0%)
- **Minor defects**: 12 samples had minor packaging damage

**Without the inspection report**, the entire shipment would have been sent to Australia, resulting in:
- Customs seizure of electrically unsafe products
- Replacement production and shipping costs
- Lost sales during the 60-day replacement cycle
- Total estimated loss: $15,000+

**With the inspection report**, the importer:
- Rejected the shipment and requested correction
- The supplier fixed the critical wiring issues and replaced defective units
- Shipment was delayed by only 7 days
- Additional inspection confirmed compliance
- Total cost of delay: $500 (re-inspection fee)

## FAQ

### How much does a Yiwu inspection report cost?
Professional inspection services in Yiwu typically cost $100–$300 per day, depending on the inspection type and product complexity. Pre-shipment inspections are the most common and cost-effective investment you can make.

### Who should conduct the inspection?
Use a third-party inspection company or your sourcing agent's inspection team. Never rely on the supplier to self-inspect, as they have a conflict of interest. Independent inspection ensures objective findings.

### When should I schedule the inspection?
Schedule pre-shipment inspection after the supplier reports production is complete and goods are packed. This is typically 1–3 days before the planned shipping date. Never inspect goods at the factory without proper preparation.

### What if the inspection fails?
If the inspection fails, you have several options: reject the entire lot, request correction and re-inspection, negotiate a price reduction, or cancel the order. Your agent or inspection company can help negotiate the best outcome.

### Can I inspect goods myself instead of hiring a professional?
While you can conduct your own inspection, professional inspectors have standardized processes, calibrated measurement tools, and experience identifying defects you might miss. For orders over $2,000, professional inspection is highly recommended.

### What is the difference between pre-shipment inspection and during-production inspection?
Pre-shipment inspection checks finished goods before they leave the factory. During-production inspection checks goods at various stages of production to catch problems early. Pre-shipment is more common and cost-effective for most orders.

### How long does a typical inspection take?
Most inspections take 4–8 hours depending on order size and product complexity. Small orders with simple products may take 2–4 hours. Large orders with complex products may take a full day.

### What should I do if the supplier refuses inspection?
Supplier refusal to allow inspection is a major red flag. Professional suppliers welcome inspection as proof of their quality commitment. If a supplier refuses, consider finding an alternative supplier.

### Should I inspect every order?
For orders over $1,000, yes. For orders under $1,000, consider the risk vs. cost. Even a single inspection can save you thousands in potential losses from defective products.

### What certifications should I verify during inspection?
Depending on your product and destination country, verify relevant certifications: CE (Europe), FCC (USA), UL (safety), RoHS (environmental), CPSIA (children's products), and any category-specific certifications required by your market.

### How do I choose an inspection company?
Look for companies with ISO 17020 accreditation, experience in your product category, coverage in your sourcing region (Yiwu, Guangzhou, Shenzhen), and transparent pricing. Popular options include SGS, Bureau Veritas, and local Yiwu-based inspection firms.

## Final Verdict

A useful Yiwu inspection report is not just a document — it is your quality assurance lifeline. By ensuring your report includes proper identification, quantity comparison, sampling methodology, defect classification, photographic evidence, and packaging verification, you protect yourself from costly quality failures. Invest in proper inspection, and it will pay for itself many times over.
