## Executive Summary

German customs clearance involves submitting documentation, paying duties and taxes, and obtaining release for free circulation. The process typically takes 1-5 business days with complete documentation. Key success factors include: accurate declarations, proper documentation, and understanding of HS codes.

**Key points:**

- Average clearance time: 1-5 business days
- Documentation errors cause 70% of delays
- ATLAS is the electronic customs system
- EORI number required for all imports
- Customs brokers reduce clearance time by 50%

## Overview of German Customs

### Structure

The German Customs (Zoll) operates under the Federal Ministry of Finance and is responsible for:

- Collecting import duties and VAT
- Enforcing trade regulations
- Controlling goods movement
- Preventing smuggling

### Key Concepts

| Concept | German Term | Description |
|---------|------------|-------------|
| Customs Clearance | Zollabfertigung | Process of releasing goods |
| Customs Declaration | Zollanmeldung | Official import declaration |
| Free Circulation | Frlauf | Goods released for domestic use |
| Customs Warehouse | Zollager | Duty-deferred storage |
| Temporary Admission | Vorübergehende Einfuhr | Temporary import |

## Step-by-Step Clearance Process

### Phase 1: Pre-Arrival Preparation

1. **Obtain EORI Number** — Register if not already done
2. **Verify Documentation** — Ensure all documents are complete
3. **Classify Products** — Determine correct HS codes
4. **Calculate Duties** — Estimate costs before arrival
5. **Choose Customs Procedure** — Standard, suspension, or warehousing

### Phase 2: Arrival and Declaration

6. **Goods Arrive** — At port, airport, or border crossing
7. **Submit Declaration** — Via ATLAS or customs broker
8. **Document Review** — Customs reviews documentation
9. **Duty Assessment** — Customs calculates duties and taxes
10. **Payment** — Pay duties and VAT via ELSTER

### Phase 3: Release and Delivery

11. **Customs Release** — Goods cleared for free circulation
12. **Pickup/Delivery** — Arrange transport to final destination
13. **Documentation** — Retain all records for 10 years

## Customs Procedures

### 1. Standard Import (Free Circulation)

Most common procedure for permanent imports:
- Pay all duties and taxes
- Goods released for domestic use
- No restrictions on use or sale

### 2. Customs Suspension

Duties suspended for processing:
- Used for goods to be processed and re-exported
- No duty paid during processing
- Duty paid only if goods enter free circulation

### 3. Customs Warehouse

Store goods without paying duties:
- Duties deferred until release
- Maximum storage: 5 years
- Useful for distribution centers

### 4. Temporary Admission

For goods temporarily in Germany:
- Reduced or no duties
- Must re-export within time limit
- Used for samples, exhibition goods

## Common Delays and Solutions

### 1. Incomplete Documentation

**Problem:** Missing or incorrect documents
**Solution:** Create checklist, verify before shipment

### 2. Incorrect HS Code

**Problem:** Wrong classification leads to delays
**Solution:** Research thoroughly, use customs broker for complex products

### 3. Value Discrepancies

**Problem:** Declared value doesn't match invoice
**Solution:** Ensure all documents show consistent values

### 4. Physical Inspection

**Problem:** Random or risk-based inspection
**Solution:** Accurate declarations reduce inspection risk

### 5. Regulatory Issues

**Problem:** Missing certifications or licenses
**Solution:** Verify requirements before shipping

## Special Customs Procedures

### Inward Processing (Veredelungsverkehr)

For goods processed in Germany and re-exported:
- Duty suspension during processing
- No duty if re-exported within time limit
- Ideal for manufacturing operations

### Outward Processing (Ausfuhrveredelung)

For goods processed abroad and returned:
- Reduced duty on re-import
- Used for textiles, electronics assembly
- Requires prior authorization

### End-Use Relief (Verwendungszoll)

For specific uses:
- Reduced duty for aerospace, shipbuilding
- Requires证明 of end-use
- Limited to authorized companies

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### 1. How long does customs clearance take?

Typically 1-5 business days with complete documentation. Complex cases may take longer.

### 2. What is an EORI number?

Economic Operators Registration and Identification number, required for all EU imports. Register once, valid EU-wide.

### 3. Can I clear customs myself?

Yes, but it's complex. For beginners or complex products, use a customs broker.

### 4. What happens if I don't pay duties?

Goods remain in customs, storage fees accumulate, and eventually goods may be auctioned.

### 5. How do I find the HS code?

Use ATLAS database, EU TARIC, or consult a customs broker.

### 6. What is ATLAS?

Germany's electronic customs declaration system. Required for all commercial imports.

### 7. Can I check clearance status online?

Yes, through ATLAS or your customs broker's portal.

### 8. What are customs warehouses?

Facilities for storing imported goods without paying duties until release.

### 9. How do I reduce customs costs?

Use correct HS codes, leverage FTAs, consolidate shipments, and optimize packaging.

### 10. What is the IOSS scheme?

Import One-Stop Shop for collecting VAT on B2C e-commerce sales under €150.

### 11. Do I need a German company to import?

Not necessarily, but you need an EORI number and may need a fiscal representative.

### 12. What are the penalties for incorrect declarations?

Fines up to 5x the unpaid duty, plus interest. Criminal prosecution for fraud.
