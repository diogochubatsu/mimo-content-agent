## Executive Summary

Choosing the right freight method from China can make or break your import business. A 1,000 kg shipment of electronics from Shanghai to Los Angeles costs $800 by sea, $3,200 by air, and $5,600 by express courier — but transit times range from 2 days (express) to 35 days (sea). Understanding when to use each mode is essential for optimizing costs, meeting delivery deadlines, and maintaining competitive pricing.

In 2026, China's freight landscape offers four primary options: sea freight (FCL and LCL), air freight (standard cargo and charter), express courier (DHL, FedEx, UPS, TNT), and rail freight (China-Europe Express). Each has distinct advantages for different shipment sizes, product types, and urgency levels. Sea freight remains 60-70% cheaper than air but requires 20-40 days of transit. Air freight offers speed (3-7 days) at a premium. Express courier provides door-to-door convenience for small parcels. Rail freight bridges the gap between sea and air for Europe-bound cargo.

This guide provides real cost data, transit times by destination, a decision framework, and case studies with actual cost breakdowns to help importers choose the optimal freight method for their specific needs.

## Section 1: Overview of Freight Options

### Sea Freight: The Workhorse of Global Trade

Sea freight handles approximately 90% of global trade by volume and remains the most cost-effective method for shipping from China. Goods travel in containers (20ft or 40ft) or as consolidated loads (LCL) on scheduled container vessels. Transit times range from 15-45 days depending on destination, with major routes connecting China's coastal ports to North America, Europe, Southeast Asia, and Australia.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Lowest cost per kilogram and per cubic meter
- Best for bulk shipments (1 CBM or 100+ kg)
- Requires container booking 2-4 weeks in advance
- Subject to port congestion and weather delays
- Full container load (FCL) offers better rates than less-than-container load (LCL)

**2026 Market Update:** Ocean freight rates have stabilized after the volatility of 2023-2025. The Shanghai Containerized Freight Index (SCFI) shows average rates of $1,800-$2,400 per 40ft container to the US West Coast, down from peaks of $10,000+ in 2021-2022.

### Air Freight: Speed at a Premium

Air freight moves goods on commercial or cargo aircraft, offering the fastest international transit times. From China's major airports (PVG, CAN, SZX, HKG), flight times to major destinations range from 10-20 hours, with total door-to-door delivery in 3-7 business days.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Fastest transit time (3-7 days door-to-door)
- Highest cost per kilogram ($4-$12/kg depending on route)
- Best for high-value, time-sensitive, or perishable goods
- Chargeable weight based on actual or volumetric weight
- Limited by airline cargo capacity and seasonal demand

**2026 Market Update:** Air cargo capacity has normalized post-pandemic. Major Chinese airlines (Air China, China Southern, China Eastern) operate dedicated freighter services on key routes, while belly capacity on passenger flights provides additional volume. Peak season (October-December) typically sees 20-30% rate increases.

### Express Courier: Door-to-Door Convenience

Express courier services (DHL, FedEx, UPS, TNT) offer integrated door-to-door delivery with customs clearance, tracking, and insurance. Best for small parcels (under 30 kg) and urgent documents, express services provide the most convenient shipping experience but at the highest cost per kilogram.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Door-to-door service with customs clearance included
- Fastest option for small parcels (1-5 days)
- Integrated tracking from pickup to delivery
- Highest cost per kilogram ($15-$45/kg)
- Weight limits (typically 30-70 kg per piece)
- DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) options available

**2026 Market Update:** Express carriers have expanded their China operations with more frequent flights and improved customs clearance capabilities. DHL and FedEx now offer 2-day service from major Chinese cities to US and European destinations.

### Rail Freight: The China-Europe Connection

Rail freight has emerged as a competitive alternative for shipping between China and Europe since the China-Europe Express Railway launched in 2011. Transit times of 14-18 days bridge the gap between sea (30-40 days) and air (3-7 days), at costs 40-60% lower than air freight.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Transit time: 14-18 days to Europe
- Cost: 40-60% cheaper than air freight
- Best for medium-value goods to European destinations
- Limited to China-Europe routes only
- Subject to capacity constraints during peak seasons
- Growing infrastructure with new routes and terminals

**2026 Market Update:** China-Europe rail services have expanded significantly, with multiple weekly departures from cities like Chengdu, Chongqing, Xi'an, and Zhengzhou. The service now covers over 50 European cities with improved reliability and customs procedures.

## Section 3: Sea Freight Deep Dive

### FCL vs LCL: Which to Choose?

**Full Container Load (FCL):**
- Exclusive use of 20ft (28 CBM) or 40ft (56 CBM) container
- Best when shipment exceeds 15 CBM or 10,000 kg
- Lower cost per CBM, faster loading/unloading
- Direct port-to-port service available
- Typical rates: $1,500-$2,400 per 40ft to US West Coast

**Less-than-Container Load (LCL):**
- Shared container space with other shippers
- Ideal for shipments under 15 CBM
- Higher per-CBM cost but lower total cost for small volumes
- Requires consolidation at origin and deconsolidation at destination
- Typical rates: $80-$150 per CBM

**Decision Rule:** If your shipment exceeds 15 CBM, FCL is almost always cheaper. Below 10 CBM, LCL is more economical. Between 10-15 CBM, compare both options with your freight forwarder.

- Transit to Rotterdam: 28-33 days

### Incoterms Explained

**FOB (Free On Board):**
- Seller delivers goods to port of loading
- Buyer responsible for freight, insurance, customs
- Most common term for sea freight
- Allows buyer to control shipping costs and carrier selection

**CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight):**
- Seller pays freight and insurance to destination port
- Buyer responsible for customs clearance and import duties
- Useful when buyer lacks shipping experience
- Limited control over carrier selection

**DDP (Delivered Duty Paid):**
- Seller responsible for all costs including customs duties
- Buyer receives goods at final destination with no additional costs
- Most convenient but highest cost
- Requires seller to understand destination country customs regulations

### Chargeable Weight Calculation

Air freight charges are based on the greater of actual weight or volumetric weight:

**Formula:** Volumetric Weight (kg) = Length (cm) × Width (cm) × Height (cm) ÷ 6,000

**Example:** A box measuring 60cm × 40cm × 40cm
- Actual weight: 20 kg
- Volumetric weight: 60 × 40 × 40 ÷ 6,000 = 16 kg
- Chargeable weight: 20 kg (actual weight is higher)

**Example:** Same box with actual weight of 10 kg
- Volumetric weight: 16 kg
- Chargeable weight: 16 kg (volumetric weight is higher)

**Pro Tip:** Optimize packaging to minimize volumetric weight. Use vacuum packaging for textiles and compress packaging where possible.

### Air Freight Restricted Items

**Prohibited Items:**
- Explosives, flammable materials, oxidizers
- Compressed gases (aerosols, propane tanks)
- Corrosive substances (batteries, chemicals)
- Radioactive materials
- Infectious substances

**Restricted Items (require special handling):**
- Lithium batteries (limited quantities, packaging requirements)
- Magnetic materials (shielding required)
- Perishable goods (temperature control required)
- Pharmaceuticals (documentation required)
- Live animals (special permits required)

**Documentation Requirements:**
- Commercial invoice
- Packing list
- Air waybill (AWB)
- Certificate of origin (if required)
- Export license (for controlled items)

### Door-to-Door Service Explained

Express courier services provide complete door-to-door delivery:
1. **Pickup** from supplier's factory or warehouse
2. **Export customs** clearance in China
3. **International transport** via air freight
4. **Import customs** clearance at destination
5. **Final delivery** to recipient's door

**Advantages:**
- Single point of contact for entire shipment
- Customs clearance included (DDP option)
- Real-time tracking from pickup to delivery
- Insurance options available
- No need to arrange separate customs broker

**Limitations:**
- Weight and size restrictions per piece
- Higher cost than traditional freight
- Limited for large or heavy items
- Remote area surcharges may apply

## Section 6: Rail Freight (China-Europe)

### Transit Time vs Cost

Rail freight offers a middle ground between sea and air:
- **Transit Time:** 14-18 days (vs 25-40 days sea, 3-7 days air)
- **Cost:** $2.50-$5.00/kg (vs $0.50-$2.00/kg sea, $4-$12/kg air)
- **Capacity:** Limited compared to sea and air
- **Reliability:** Moderate (subject to border delays and capacity constraints)

### Route Options

**Western Route (Most Popular):**
- Origin: Chengdu, Chongqing, Xi'an, Zhengzhou
- Transit through: Kazakhstan, Russia, Belarus
- Destination: Poland (Małaszewicze), Germany (Hamburg, Duisburg)
- Transit Time: 14-16 days

**Northern Route:**
- Origin: Harbin, Manzhouli
- Transit through: Russia, Finland
- Destination: Finland, Scandinavia
- Transit Time: 12-14 days

**Southern Route:**
- Origin: Kunming, Guangzhou
- Transit through: Vietnam, Kazakhstan
- Destination: Central Asia, Eastern Europe
- Transit Time: 18-22 days

### China-Europe Rail Advantages

**Cost Efficiency:** 40-60% cheaper than air freight for similar transit times
**Speed:** 10-20 days faster than sea freight
**Reliability:** Fixed schedules and routes
**Sustainability:** Lower carbon footprint than air freight
**Capacity:** Growing infrastructure with new terminals

### Limitations

- Only available for China-Europe routes
- Subject to capacity constraints during peak seasons
- Border delays at Kazakhstan-Russia and Russia-Belarus crossings
- Limited cargo types (no hazardous materials on most services)
- Growing but still developing network

## Section 7: Decision Framework

### When to Use Sea Freight

**Use sea freight when:**
- Shipment exceeds 1 CBM or 100 kg
- Delivery deadline is flexible (30+ days acceptable)
- Products are low-value per kilogram
- Bulk orders from suppliers
- Seasonal inventory with planned lead times
- Shipping heavy or bulky items (furniture, machinery)

**Avoid sea freight when:**
- Delivery is time-sensitive (under 14 days required)
- Products are high-value or perishable
- Small quantities or samples needed quickly
- Market conditions require rapid inventory replenishment

### When to Use Air Freight

**Use air freight when:**
- Delivery required within 3-7 days
- Products are high-value (>$50/kg)
- Time-sensitive inventory (fashion, electronics)
- Perishable goods or medical supplies
- Small to medium quantities (10-500 kg)
- Market demand is uncertain and speed matters

**Avoid air freight when:**
- Shipment is large and heavy (>500 kg)
- Products are low-value per kilogram
- Delivery can wait 20+ days
- Budget is extremely tight

### When to Use Express Courier

**Use express courier when:**
- Shipment under 30 kg
- Door-to-door service required
- Customs clearance assistance needed
- Small parcels or documents
- Urgent delivery (1-5 days)
- E-commerce orders or samples

**Avoid express courier when:**
- Shipment exceeds 30 kg per piece
- Large or bulky items
- Cost is primary concern
- Bulk shipments needed

### When to Use Rail Freight

**Use rail freight when:**
- Shipping to European destinations
- Transit time of 14-18 days is acceptable
- Cost savings vs air freight needed
- Medium-value goods ($10-$50/kg)
- Regular shipments to Europe
- Sustainability is a priority

**Avoid rail freight when:**
- Destination is not in Europe
- Transit time must be under 14 days
- Shipment is extremely large (use sea instead)
- Budget allows for air freight speed

## FAQ

**Q1: What is the cheapest way to ship from China?**
A: Sea freight is the cheapest method for shipments over 1 CBM, with costs of $0.50-$2.00/kg. For small parcels under 30 kg, express courier can be competitive when considering total cost including customs clearance.

**Q2: How long does sea freight take from China to the US?**
A: Sea freight from China to the US West Coast takes 14-18 days, while East Coast destinations take 25-30 days. Add 3-5 days for customs clearance and local delivery.

**Q3: Is air freight worth the extra cost?**
A: Air freight is worth the premium when time is critical, products are high-value (>$50/kg), or inventory turnover justifies the faster delivery. For low-value, bulk items, sea freight is more economical.

**Q4: What is DDP shipping and when should I use it?**
A: DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) means the seller pays all costs including customs duties. Use DDP when you want predictable landed costs and don't want to manage customs clearance. It's common for e-commerce and small business imports.

**Q5: How do I calculate volumetric weight for air freight?**
A: Volumetric weight = Length (cm) × Width (cm) × Height (cm) ÷ 6,000. The chargeable weight is the greater of actual weight or volumetric weight.

**Q6: What are the restrictions for shipping lithium batteries?**
A: Lithium batteries require special packaging, labeling, and documentation. They must be shipped under specific UN3480 or UN3481 classifications. Quantity limits apply, and some batteries may be restricted on passenger aircraft.

**Q7: Can I track my shipment in real-time?**
A: Express courier services (DHL, FedEx, UPS) offer real-time tracking from pickup to delivery. Air and sea freight provide tracking through freight forwarders, though updates may be less frequent.

**Q8: What insurance options are available?**
A: Marine cargo insurance covers sea freight, air cargo insurance covers air freight, and rail cargo insurance covers rail shipments. Typical coverage is 0.3-1.0% of cargo value. Express courier services include basic coverage with options to purchase additional insurance.

**Q9: How do I choose between FCL and LCL sea freight?**
A: Choose FCL when your shipment exceeds 15 CBM or 10,000 kg. Choose LCL for smaller shipments under 15 CBM. Between 10-15 CBM, compare both options with your freight forwarder.

**Q10: What is the China-Europe rail freight and when should I use it?**
A: China-Europe rail freight offers 14-18 day transit times at costs 40-60% lower than air freight. Use it for medium-value goods to European destinations when sea freight is too slow but air freight is too expensive.

**Q11: What documents do I need for importing from China?**
A: Essential documents include commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading (sea) or air waybill (air), certificate of origin, and import license (if required). DDP shipments may require fewer documents from the buyer.

**Q12: How do peak seasons affect shipping costs?**
A: Peak season (October-December) sees 20-30% rate increases for air freight and surcharges for sea freight. Plan shipments in advance and consider booking 4-6 weeks ahead during peak periods.
