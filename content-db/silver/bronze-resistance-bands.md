## Tips

1. **Material quality matters** — Latex bands are cheaper but can snap. Fabric bands cost 2-3x more but are safer and more durable. Target premium market with fabric.

2. **Set composition sells** — 5-band sets (different resistance levels) sell better than single bands. Include a carry bag and exercise guide for perceived value.

3. **Color coding is essential** — Customers expect color-coded resistance levels (yellow=light, red=medium, green=heavy). Always specify colors in orders.

4. **Packaging adds value** — Custom packaging with brand logo costs $0.20-0.50 extra but allows 2-3x price increase. Include exercise chart inside.

5. **Seasonal peaks** — Sales spike in January (New Year resolutions) and March (summer body prep). Plan inventory 2 months ahead.

## FAQ

**Q: What's the difference between latex and fabric resistance bands?**
A: Latex bands are cheaper ($0.30-0.80) but can snap and cause injuries. Fabric bands ($1.50-3.50) are non-slip, more durable, and safer. For premium markets, always choose fabric.

**Q: What resistance levels should I include?**
A: Standard 5-band sets include: X-Light (5-10 lbs), Light (10-20 lbs), Medium (20-30 lbs), Heavy (30-40 lbs), X-Heavy (40-50 lbs). This covers all fitness levels.

**Q: How do I test quality before bulk ordering?**
A: Order 2-3 sample sets. Test: stretch recovery (should return to original shape), snap resistance (stretch 3x length), and color fastness (wash 5 times). Poor quality bands lose elasticity quickly.

**Q: What certifications are needed?**
A: Resistance bands don't require specific certifications for most markets. However, for EU, ensure compliance with REACH regulations (no harmful chemicals). For US, general product safety standards apply.

**Q: Can I sell these as "fitness equipment"?**
A: Yes, but be specific. Use keywords like "resistance bands for exercise," "workout bands," "physical therapy bands." Different audiences: fitness enthusiasts, physical therapy patients, home gym owners.
