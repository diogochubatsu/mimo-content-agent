## Executive Summary

Shipping from China remains one of the most critical decisions for importers and e-commerce sellers in 2026. With global supply chains stabilizing after years of disruption, businesses now have more options than ever—but choosing the wrong method can erase profit margins or delay product launches by weeks.

The three primary shipping methods from China are sea freight, air freight, and express courier services. Sea freight offers the lowest per-unit cost but requires 25-45 days transit time. Air freight balances speed (3-7 days) with moderate costs. Express couriers like DHL, FedEx, and UPS provide door-to-door delivery in 3-5 days at premium rates.

In 2026, new regulations around carbon emissions reporting, digital customs declarations, and e-commerce simplified clearance thresholds are reshaping the landscape. Importers must stay current with these changes to avoid unexpected fees and compliance issues.

This guide covers every aspect of shipping from China—from selecting the right method based on your cargo type and timeline to navigating customs clearance in your destination country. Whether you're shipping a single pallet or filling containers, you'll find actionable insights and real cost data to make informed decisions.

## Section 1: Understanding Shipping Methods

### The Three Pillars of China Shipping

Every shipment from China falls into one of three categories. Understanding the fundamental differences helps you match your logistics needs with the right service.

**Sea Freight** handles the vast majority of China's exports by volume. It uses container vessels operating on established trade routes to major ports worldwide. This method is ideal for large, heavy shipments where cost per kilogram matters more than delivery speed.

**Air Freight** moves cargo through China's international airports—primarily Shanghai Pudong (PVG), Beijing Capital (PEK), and Guangzhou Baiyun (CAN). It's the middle ground between speed and cost, suitable for high-value or time-sensitive goods that don't justify express courier premiums.

**Express Courier** services provide complete door-to-door logistics with tracking. Companies like DHL, FedEx, UPS, and SF Express handle everything from pickup at the factory to final-mile delivery, including customs brokerage built into the service.

### Choosing Between Methods

Your shipping method should align with four factors: shipment weight, value density, urgency, and destination. A 20kg box of electronics samples warrants different treatment than 5,000 units of phone cases bound for Amazon FBA.

## Section 2: Sea Freight in Detail

### FCL vs LCL: Container Options

Sea freight divides into two primary service levels:

**Full Container Load (FCL):** You rent an entire container. Standard sizes are 20-foot (TEU) holding approximately 28 CBM or 40-foot (FEU) holding approximately 58 CBM. FCL offers better per-unit pricing and faster handling since containers go directly from origin port to destination port.

**Less than Container Load (LCL):** Your cargo shares container space with other shippers. You pay only for the volume you use, measured in cubic meters. LCL is practical for shipments under 15 CBM, though it adds handling time at consolidation points.

LCL rates typically run $60-$120 per CBM depending on route and carrier, plus origin and destination handling fees of $150-$300 each.

### Key Sea Freight Considerations

- **Booking lead time:** Book 2-4 weeks before desired departure date
- **Seasonal peaks:** Rates spike 20-40% during pre-Lunar New Year (January-February) and pre-Christmas (August-October) periods
- **Port congestion:** Monitor port status; congestion can add 3-7 days to transit
- **Container type:** Standard dry containers work for most goods; reefer containers needed for perishables; open-top for oversized cargo

## Section 3: Air Freight in Detail

### Air Freight Service Levels

Air freight offers three service tiers:

**Standard Air Freight:** Consolidated shipments on scheduled cargo flights. Transit time is 5-8 days including customs processing. Cost-effective for shipments between 45kg and 300kg.

**Express Air Freight:** Priority handling on passenger or dedicated cargo flights. Transit time drops to 3-5 days. Premium of 30-50% over standard rates.

**Charter Services:** Dedicated aircraft for oversized or extremely urgent cargo. Reserved for shipments exceeding 5,000kg or requiring guaranteed next-day delivery.

### When Air Freight Makes Sense

Air freight is optimal when your cargo value exceeds $15/kg, you need inventory within one week, or you're shipping perishable items. The premium over sea freight typically pays for itself through faster inventory turnover and reduced warehouse holding costs.

## Section 4: Express Courier Options

### Major Couriers Serving China

**DHL International:** Strongest network in Europe and Asia. Typical transit: 3-5 days worldwide. Best for documents and parcels under 30kg. DHL Express China operates hubs in Shanghai, Guangzhou, and Shenzhen.

**FedEx International:** Dominant in North America. FedEx IP (International Priority) delivers in 2-4 days to major US cities. FedEx IE (International Economy) offers 4-7 day service at 20-30% lower rates.

**UPS Worldwide:** Excellent coverage in the Americas and Europe. UPS Worldwide Express: 1-3 days. UPS Worldwide Expedited: 2-5 days. Strong customs brokerage capabilities.

**SF Express:** China's premier domestic courier with growing international network. Competitive rates for shipments originating in China. SF International handles customs clearance efficiently.

**TNT (FedEx subsidiary):** Strong European presence, particularly Benelux and Germany. Integrated with FedEx since 2016 but maintains separate booking systems.

## Section 5: Customs Clearance

### Understanding Import Duties

Every country levies duties on imported goods. Key concepts:

**HS Code (Harmonized System):** International standard for classifying traded products. Your HS code determines duty rates. Accurate classification is your responsibility—errors can result in penalties or shipment delays.

**Customs Value:** The declared value of goods, typically the transaction value (what you paid the supplier). Includes cost of goods, international shipping, and insurance.

**Duty Rate:** Calculated as a percentage of customs value. Rates vary by product category and origin country. China-origin goods face standard Most Favored Nation (MFN) rates in WTO countries.

**Value Added Tax (VAT) / Goods and Services Tax (GST):** Applied to customs value plus duty. Rates vary: 20% UK, 19% Germany, 10% Australia, varies in US by state.

### Customs Clearance Process

1. **Commercial Invoice:** Supplier provides invoice showing goods description, quantities, unit prices, and total value
2. **Packing List:** Details contents of each package, weight, and dimensions
3. **Bill of Lading / Air Waybill:** Transport document proving shipment
4. **Certificate of Origin:** Proves goods were manufactured in China (may affect duty rates under trade agreements)
5. **Product Certifications:** CE marking, FCC, UL, or other standards compliance documents as required

### Duty Avoidance Strategies

- **Trade agreements:** Check if your country has preferential duty rates with China
- **De minimis thresholds:** Many countries exempt low-value shipments from duties (US: $800, EU: €150, UK: £135)
- **Bonded warehouses:** Store goods without paying duties until they enter domestic market
- **Foreign Trade Zones (FTZ):** US FTZs allow duty deferral or reduction on re-exported goods

## When to Use Each Method

### Choose Sea Freight When:
- Shipment weight exceeds 500kg
- Delivery timeline allows 4+ weeks
- Product value is under $5/kg
- You're restocking established inventory
- Filling Amazon FBA containers

### Choose Air Freight When:
- Shipment is 50-500kg
- You need goods within 1 week
- Product value exceeds $15/kg
- Launching new product lines quickly
- Seasonal demand requires fast restocking

### Choose Express Courier When:
- Shipment is under 30kg
- Urgency requires 3-5 day delivery
- Shipping samples or prototypes
- E-commerce orders requiring tracking
- Documents or small high-value items

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Prepare Your Shipment
Work with your supplier to create accurate commercial invoices and packing lists. Ensure product descriptions match actual contents—customs authorities penalize mismatches.

### Step 2: Select Your Shipping Method
Use the comparison tables above to match your shipment characteristics with the optimal method. Consider total landed cost, not just freight charges.

### Step 3: Book with a Freight Forwarder
Unless shipping express, engage a freight forwarder. Compare quotes from 3-5 forwarders. Check their experience with your specific product category and destination.

### Step 4: Arrange Customs Documentation
Prepare or verify: commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or air waybill, certificate of origin, and any required product certifications.

### Step 5: Track and Receive
Monitor your shipment through the carrier's tracking system. Arrange customs clearance at destination before arrival to prevent port storage charges.

## Case Study

### Background
TechGear Solutions, a US-based electronics distributor, needed to import 5,000 units of wireless earbuds from Shenzhen to their Los Angeles warehouse. The shipment weighed 750kg total, with a product value of $12 per unit ($60,000 total).

### Challenge
The company faced a tight timeline: a major retail launch was scheduled for September 15, and the earbuds needed to be in FBA by September 1. They also needed to maintain a target landed cost under $18 per unit.

### Solution
TechGear compared three options:
- **Sea freight (FCL):** $4,800 total, 22-day transit. Arrives September 3—too late.
- **Air freight:** $5,250 total (750kg × $7.00/kg), 5-day transit. Arrives August 15. Within budget.
- **Express DHL:** $9,000+ total, 3-day transit. Over budget.

They selected air freight, booking two weeks in advance through a freight forwarder who consolidated with another shipper's cargo for a 10% discount.

### Results
- Total shipping cost: $4,725 ($6.30/kg after discount)
- Landed cost per unit: $12.00 (product) + $0.95 (freight) + $2.10 (duties/tax) + $0.45 (customs) = **$15.50 per unit**
- Shipment arrived August 14, one day ahead of schedule
- Products were in FBA by August 20, meeting the September 1 deadline
- Retail launch proceeded on schedule

### Key Takeaways
- Air freight's higher per-kg cost was offset by avoiding late inventory penalties
- Booking early secured better rates and guaranteed capacity
- Using a freight forwarder's consolidation service saved 10-15% versus direct airline booking
- Accurate HS code classification prevented customs delays

## FAQ

**Q: How long does sea freight from China to the US take in 2026?**
A: Sea freight transit from major Chinese ports (Shanghai, Shenzhen, Ningbo) to US West Coast ports (Los Angeles, Long Beach, Seattle) typically takes 14-20 days. East Coast destinations (New York, Savannah) add 8-12 additional days, totaling 22-32 days. Transit times can vary based on carrier schedules, port congestion, and weather conditions.

**Q: What is the cheapest way to ship from China?**
A: Sea freight LCL (Less than Container Load) offers the lowest cost for shipments under 15 CBM, typically $60-$120 per CBM. For larger shipments, FCL (Full Container Load) provides the best per-unit economics. Express courier services are the most expensive option per kilogram but offer the fastest delivery and simplest process.

**Q: Do I need a freight forwarder to ship from China?**
A: While not legally required, using a freight forwarder is strongly recommended. Forwarders handle carrier negotiations, documentation, customs clearance coordination, and cargo insurance. They typically charge 10-15% of freight cost but save time and prevent costly errors, especially for first-time importers.

**Q: What are the customs duties for importing from China?**
A: Duty rates depend on your product's HS code and destination country. US MFN rates for Chinese goods typically range from 0-25%, with many electronics at 0-4%. EU rates vary by product category, commonly 0-14%. Anti-dumping duties may apply to specific product categories like steel, solar panels, or certain electronics.

**Q: Can I ship lithium batteries from China?**
A: Yes, but lithium batteries have strict IATA and IMO regulations. Batteries must be shipped via UN38.3-tested packaging, with proper labeling and documentation. Sea freight is generally preferred for large quantities since air freight restrictions on lithium batteries are more severe. Many freight forwarders specialize in battery shipments.

**Q: What happens if my shipment is held at customs?**
A: Customs holds occur due to documentation errors, suspected undervaluation, missing certifications, or random inspection. Resolution typically takes 2-7 days. Provide requested documents promptly, and consider hiring a licensed customs broker to expedite clearance. Prevention through accurate documentation is always faster than resolution.

**Q: How do I calculate volumetric weight for air freight?**
A: Volumetric weight equals (Length × Width × Height in cm) ÷ 6000. Airlines charge the greater of actual weight or volumetric weight. For example, a box measuring 50×40×30 cm has a volumetric weight of 10 kg. If the actual weight is 7 kg, you're charged for 10 kg.

**Q: What insurance do I need for shipping from China?**
A: Cargo insurance typically covers 110% of CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight) value at rates of 0.3-0.5% of declared value. Marine cargo insurance covers sea freight; air cargo insurance covers air shipments. Express couriers include basic coverage (usually $100-$200) but recommend additional insurance for high-value goods.
