## Section 1: Understanding Amazon FBA Fee Structure

Amazon's FBA fee structure in 2026 is designed to charge sellers based on the resources their products consume in Amazon's fulfillment network. The system uses product dimensions, weight, and category to determine applicable fees.

### The Seven Fee Categories

**1. Fulfillment Fees**
The core cost of having Amazon pick, pack, and ship your products. These fees are based on product size tier (small standard, large standard, small oversize, etc.) and unit weight. Fulfillment fees increase annually, with the most recent adjustment taking effect in January 2026.

**2. Monthly Storage Fees**
Charged per cubic foot of warehouse space your inventory occupies. Rates vary by season: higher during Q4 (October-December) when warehouse space is at premium, lower during the rest of the year. Location also affects pricing, with fulfillment centers in high-cost areas charging more.

**3. Long-Term Storage Fees (LTSF)**
Designed to discourage slow-moving inventory, these fees apply to units stored for 181-365 days ($3.80/unit or $0.15/cubic foot) and 365+ days ($6.90/unit or $0.15/cubic foot). Monthly assessments begin on the 15th of each month.

**4. Referral Fees**
Category-dependent percentages charged on each sale, typically 8-17%. These are separate from FBA fees and apply to both FBA and FBM sellers. Some categories have minimum referral fees.

**5. Prep Fees**
Charges for products requiring special handling: poly bagging ($0.55), bubble wrapping ($0.60), taping ($0.30), and other preparation services. Sellers can reduce costs by prepping products before shipping to Amazon.

**6. Return Processing Fees**
Applied to certain categories (clothing, jewelry, watches, etc.) to cover the cost of processing customer returns. Ranges from $0.20 to $2.00+ depending on category and product size.

**7. Miscellaneous Fees**
Includes labeling ($0.55/unit), removal orders ($0.50-$1.00/unit), disposal ($0.50/unit), and Amazon-managed transport for multi-channel fulfillment.

### How Fees Are Calculated

Amazon calculates fees using a tiered system based on product dimensions and weight:

- **Small Standard**: Up to 15 oz, dimensions within 15" x 12" x 0.75"
- **Large Standard**: Up to 20 lb, dimensions within 18" x 14" x 8"
- **Small Oversize**: Up to 70 lb, dimensions within 18" x 14" x 8"
- **Medium Oversize**: Up to 150 lb, dimensions within same limits
- **Large Oversize**: Up to 150 lb, dimensions up to 108" x 84" x 84"
- **Special Oversize**: Over 150 lb or exceeding large oversize dimensions

The fee is determined by whichever dimension (length, width, height) results in the highest charge—either dimensional weight or actual weight, whichever is greater.

### Dimensional Weight Calculation

Amazon uses dimensional weight for large standard and oversize items. The formula is:

**Dimensional Weight (lb) = (Length × Width × Height) / 166**

If dimensional weight exceeds actual weight, Amazon charges based on dimensional weight. This is crucial for lightweight but bulky products.

### Long-Term Storage Fees

| Inventory Age | Fee |
|---------------|-----|

| 271-365 days | $3.80 per unit OR $0.15 per cubic foot |

### Inventory Performance Index (IPI) Impact

Amazon's IPI score (0-1000) directly impacts storage limits:

- **IPI 450+**: No storage limits
- **IPI 350-449**: Storage limits apply based on category
- **IPI below 350**: Severe restrictions, potential removal orders

Maintaining a high IPI score through fast sell-through, low excess inventory, and low stockout rates is essential for avoiding storage limitations.

### Location-Based Storage Fee Variations

Storage fees vary by fulfillment center region:

- **High-cost locations** (California, New Jersey): +10-20% above standard rates
- **Standard locations** (Texas, Nevada): Standard rates
- **Low-cost locations** (Indiana, Kentucky): -5-10% below standard rates

Amazon uses remote fulfillment (FBA Remote) to optimize costs, but sellers have limited control over which warehouse their inventory is assigned to.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Calculate Your Total Landed Cost

Before considering FBA fees, determine your complete cost to get products to Amazon warehouses:

1. **Product cost**: Price paid to supplier (1688, Alibaba, etc.)
2. **Shipping to Amazon**: International freight (air/sea), customs, duties
3. **Product prep**: Any labeling, packaging, or preparation required
4. **Inspection costs**: Quality control and verification

### Step 2: Use Amazon's FBA Revenue Calculator

Navigate to Seller Central → Reports → Fulfillment → Revenue Calculator:

1. Enter product ASIN or dimensions
2. Input your sale price
3. Review the complete fee breakdown
4. Compare FBA vs FBM costs
5. Export the calculation for your records

### Step 3: Factor in All Fee Categories

Don't overlook secondary fees:

- **Referral fees**: Category-dependent (8-20%)
- **Prep fees**: If products need special handling
- **Return processing**: For clothing, jewelry, and other high-return categories
- **Storage fees**: Calculate based on expected sell-through rate

### Step 4: Set Profitable Price Points

Using your total cost calculation:

1. **Determine minimum profitable price**: Total cost × 2.5-3.0x markup
2. **Research competitor pricing**: Don't price yourself out of the market
3. **Consider promotional costs**: Advertising, deals, coupons
4. **Build in margin for unexpected fees**: Returns, damage, adjustments

### Step 5: Optimize Packaging and Dimensions

The single most impactful cost reduction strategy:

1. **Measure accurately**: Include all packaging dimensions
2. **Test different packaging options**: Smaller boxes, different materials
3. **Consider product bundling**: Sometimes larger units reduce per-unit fees
4. **Document dimensional weight**: Know when Amazon will use it vs actual weight

**Q: What is dimensional weight and how does it affect fees?**
A: Dimensional weight is a pricing technique used by carriers to account for lightweight but bulky packages. Amazon calculates it as (Length × Width × Height) / 166. If dimensional weight exceeds actual weight, Amazon charges based on dimensional weight. This is especially important for products with lots of air space in packaging.

**Q: How can I reduce my FBA fees?**
A: Top strategies include: (1) optimizing packaging to fit smaller size tiers, (2) maintaining fast inventory turnover to avoid long-term storage fees, (3) pre-packing products to avoid prep fees, (4) ensuring correct category placement for optimal referral fees, (5) improving your IPI score to avoid storage limits, and (6) regular fee audits to identify unexpected charges.

**Q: What is the Inventory Performance Index (IPI) and why does it matter?**
A: IPI is Amazon's score (0-1000) measuring how efficiently you manage inventory. It considers: sell-through rate, excess inventory percentage, stranded inventory, and stockout rate. A score of 450+ avoids storage limits. Below 350, you face severe restrictions. Maintaining high IPI is crucial for scaling your FBA business.

**Q: Are there ways to avoid long-term storage fees?**
A: Yes. Strategies include: (1) maintaining accurate demand forecasting, (2) creating removal orders before the 181-day threshold, (3) liquidating slow-moving inventory, (4) using Amazon Outlet for clearance, (5) running promotions to boost sell-through, and (6) monitoring inventory age reports weekly. The key is proactive management—don't let inventory age past 180 days.

**Q: How do referral fees differ from FBA fees?**
A: Referral fees are category-dependent percentages charged on each sale (8-20%) and apply to both FBA and FBM sellers. FBA fees are specifically for Amazon's fulfillment services (picking, packing, shipping). Both apply simultaneously—you pay referral fees regardless of fulfillment method, plus FBA fees if using Fulfillment by Amazon.

**Q: What happens if my IPI score drops below 450?**
A: If your IPI drops below 450, Amazon imposes storage limits on your account. Below 350, limits become more severe and you may face removal orders for excess inventory. To improve your IPI: increase sell-through rate, reduce excess inventory, fix stranded listings, and avoid stockouts on best-selling products.

**Q: Can I negotiate FBA fees with Amazon?**
A: Individual sellers cannot negotiate standard FBA fees, but high-volume sellers (typically $1M+ annual revenue) may qualify for volume discounts through Amazon's Account Manager program. Additionally, the FBA New Selection program offers fee reductions for newly launched products.
