## Why Budget Planning Matters

Without proper budget planning, importers often:
- **Run out of capital** mid-order, forcing compromises on quality
- **Underestimate total costs**, leading to negative margins
- **Over-invest in inventory**, tying up cash that could be used elsewhere
- **Miss opportunities** because funds are allocated incorrectly
- **Face cash flow crises** when unexpected costs arise

## Budget Planning Tips

### Start Small and Scale
Begin with the minimum viable budget to test products and processes. Once you have proven results, scale up your investment.

### Reserve Cash for Reorders
Don't invest your entire budget in the first order. Reserve 20–30% for reorders of successful products.

### Account for Timing
Cash will be tied up during production and shipping (30–60 days). Plan your cash flow accordingly.

### Include a Buffer
Add 10–15% buffer to your budget for unexpected costs. This is especially important for first-time importers.

### Track Every Cost
Keep detailed records of all expenses. This helps you calculate accurate margins and plan future budgets.

## FAQ

### What is the minimum budget to start importing from Yiwu?
For first-time importers, a minimum budget of $3,000–$5,000 is recommended. This covers product costs, shipping, agent fees, and a buffer for unexpected expenses. Starting smaller is possible but limits your product options.

### How much should I allocate for shipping?
Shipping typically represents 15–30% of your total budget. For air freight, expect $5–$10/kg. For sea freight, expect $80–$150/CBM for LCL or $2,000–$4,000 for a full container.

### Can I import from Yiwu with less than $1,000?
It's possible for very small test orders, but the cost per unit will be higher due to fixed costs (agent fees, inspection, shipping minimums). For orders under $1,000, consider Alibaba with Trade Assurance.

### How do I reduce my total budget?
Strategies include: negotiating lower MOQs, choosing lighter/cheaper products, using sea freight instead of air, consolidating orders, and building relationships for better pricing over time.

### Should I include a buffer in my budget?
Yes. Always add 10–15% buffer for unexpected costs. First-time importers should add 20% buffer due to higher uncertainty.

### How long will my capital be tied up?
From order to delivery, expect 30–60 days. Add 15–30 days for selling inventory. Total cash cycle: 45–90 days.

### What if I run out of budget mid-order?
Avoid this by planning carefully. If you run short, you may need to cancel part of the order, negotiate delayed payment, or find additional funding. Prevention is better than cure.

### Can I split my budget across multiple orders?
Yes. Many importers split their budget across multiple smaller orders to reduce risk and test different products. This is recommended for first-time importers.

### How do I calculate my expected ROI?
ROI = (Revenue - Total Costs) ÷ Total Costs × 100. Factor in all costs including products, shipping, fees, duties, and platform fees.

### Should I invest more in my first order?
No. Start small, test the process, and learn from any mistakes. Scale up only after you have proven results and confidence in your sourcing process.
