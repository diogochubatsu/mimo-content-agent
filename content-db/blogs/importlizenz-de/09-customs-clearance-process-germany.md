# Customs Clearance Process for Shipping to Germany

## What Happens During Customs Clearance

When a shipment arrives, the importer or their appointed customs clearance agent submits an import declaration to the German customs authorities.

**The declaration includes:**
- Details about the goods
- Value of the goods
- Origin of the goods
- Tariff classification

Customs will then either:
1. Release the goods
2. Request additional documentation
3. Carry out a physical inspection

## Required Documentation

- Commercial invoice with full description and declared value
- Packing list
- Bill of lading or airway bill (waybill)
- Certificate of origin (where applicable)
- Import licenses or permits for restricted items
- Insurance documents

## Customs Declarations and Waybills

The customs declaration is the formal submission that triggers the clearance process. It must accurately reflect the total value, quantity, and nature of the goods.

The waybill (airway bill for air freight, bill of lading for sea freight) serves as the transport document required alongside the declaration.

**Errors or inconsistencies** between invoice, waybill, and declaration are a common cause of shipment delays.

## Restricted Items Subject to Additional Checks

- Food products (phytosanitary or veterinary checks)
- Chemicals and hazardous materials
- Electrical/electronic goods (CE compliance documentation)
- CITES-regulated items (wildlife and plant products)

## Clearance Timeline

Typically 1–3 working days. Inspections or incomplete paperwork can cause delays.

## Best Practices

- Use a customs broker for accurate tariff classifications
- Ensure all documentation is complete before arrival
- Factor in additional clearance time for restricted categories
