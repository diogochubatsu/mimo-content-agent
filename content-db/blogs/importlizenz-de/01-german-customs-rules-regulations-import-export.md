# German Customs Rules and Regulations for Import and Export

## Overview

Navigating German customs rules and regulations is essential for any business engaged in international trade. Germany sits at the heart of Europe's logistics network, making it one of the continent's busiest hubs for import and export activity.

## Key Points

### Customs Framework
- Germany operates under the EU Union Customs Code (UCC)
- Goods moving within the EU face no duties
- Goods from non-EU countries are subject to the Combined Nomenclature (CN) tariff system

### Import Requirements
- Every shipment entering Germany from outside the EU must be accompanied by a customs declaration
- Declarations submitted electronically through the ATLAS system
- Essential documentation: commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin, import licenses

### Duty Calculation
- Import duties calculated based on CIF value (transaction price plus transport and insurance)
- Specific rate determined by CN classification code
- Import VAT at 19% (reduced to 7% for select items)

### Export Controls
- Germany has rigorous export control framework
- Governed by EU Regulation 2021/821 on dual-use items
- Foreign Trade and Payments Act (AWG) and ordinance (AWV)
- BAFA handles licensing applications

### Compliance Procedures
1. Classify goods accurately using Combined Nomenclature
2. Calculate total customs value including freight and insurance
3. Prepare all documentation before goods reach border
4. Submit electronic declarations via ATLAS
5. Verify required permits and certificates
6. Screen transactions against sanctions lists
7. Maintain comprehensive records for audits

### Restricted Goods
- Medicinal imports require BfArM authorization
- Textiles must carry fiber composition labels
- Weapons, chemicals, and CITES-regulated species require special permits
- AEO (Authorized Economic Operator) status for faster clearance
